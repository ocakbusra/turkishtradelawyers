Exiting a Turkish Investment: Share Sales, Dividend Distributions and Liquidation Compared

Investment Law & Transactions November 6, 2026 5 min read

A share sale, dividend distribution and liquidation achieve different objectives. Selling shares transfers the investment to a buyer; a dividend returns distributable value while the investor remains a shareholder; liquidation winds up the company through a formal process. Choose the objective before comparing tax or timing. The route must fit the company’s financial position and commitments.

Share sale: transfer the investment

Review transfer formalities for the company form, contractual restrictions, pre-emption and required approvals. Identify change-of-control provisions in financing, licences and major contracts. Assess competition filing requirements using current rules. A buyer will examine historic liabilities, so prepare a coherent data room and resolve avoidable documentation gaps. Agree price mechanics and continuing seller exposure rather than focusing only on the headline price.

Dividend: distribute value without ending ownership

Confirm distributable amounts, required corporate decisions and any financing restrictions. Cash in a bank account is not automatically distributable profit. Obtain tax and treaty advice based on the actual recipient, documentation and payment date. Coordinate the bank process and accounting records. A dividend may meet an investor’s cash-return objective, but it does not release ownership, governance responsibilities or exposure associated with the investment.

Liquidation: close the business through a process

Assess whether ordinary liquidation is appropriate given the company’s solvency and liabilities. Plan the required corporate and registry steps, creditor treatment, asset realisation, tax matters and record retention. Employment, leases and customer commitments need separate handling. Do not promise an immediate distribution of all cash: creditors and procedural requirements must be addressed before final value can be returned.

Compare net proceeds and residual obligations

Model transaction costs, taxes, debt discharge, guarantees and retained claims for each realistic route. Identify personal or group guarantees that survive a share sale unless released. For liquidation, consider contingent liabilities and the cost of maintaining records and representation. Compare timing using evidence from the actual process rather than a generic estimate. The highest gross price may not produce the best net outcome.

Prepare the exit before it becomes urgent

Maintain clean corporate records, rights documentation and reconciled related-party balances. Resolve ownership of intellectual property and critical contracts. Establish a plan for access, data, staff communication and post-exit cooperation. If a buyer is involved, agree which records remain accessible for tax and claims purposes. Early preparation gives the investor options; a rushed exit can turn minor record gaps into price reductions or delays.

Investor checklist

  1. Clear objective: transfer, distribution or closure.
  2. Corporate, contractual and regulatory requirements.
  3. Recipient-specific tax and banking review.
  4. Creditor, guarantee and contingent-liability analysis.
  5. Documented handover and residual obligations.

Frequently asked questions

Does a dividend end the investment?

No. The investor ordinarily remains a shareholder.

Does selling shares automatically release guarantees?

No. Obtain any required express release from the beneficiary.

Is liquidation always available as a quick cash exit?

No. Solvency, creditor rights and formal procedures must be assessed.
