EU Product Liability for Goods from Türkiye from 9 December 2026

International Trade & Product Compliance November 23, 2026 9 min read

A Dutch importer receives two outwardly identical kettles from its Turkish supplier. Both were assembled in November. The first entered the EU market on 8 December 2026, the second on 9 December. Months later, a buyer reports that a handle came away while she was pouring hot water. Which liability rules will a court apply? Procurement can help answer that question today by tying each appliance to evidence of its first supply. An invoice for the entire order may leave the most important fact unresolved.

Why a single day matters

The European Parliament and Council adopted the revised Product Liability Directive on 23 October 2024. It appeared in the Official Journal on 18 November and entered into force on 8 December 2024. Member States have until 9 December 2026 to transpose it. A May 2026 corrigendum changed the boundary in Article 2. Products first placed on the Union market or put into service from 9 December 2026 fall within the new framework. Article 21 preserves the earlier regime for products placed on the market or put into service before that date. Check the implementing legislation in the relevant Member State when assessing a particular claim.

Older presentations may still reproduce the original wording and put the second kettle on the wrong side of the line. Replace those slides. Have the person who maintains the import register explain which event each date field describes. An order acknowledges a commercial commitment. A packing list identifies a consignment. A retail receipt establishes a downstream sale. Each document can contribute evidence, but those dates may refer to different transactions. A contractual promise to manufacture future units tells you little about when a particular completed unit was first made available in the Union.

Consider a Belgian distributor holding December stock for several retailers. It receives three containers and sells individual kettles in January. If the December supply was the first making available on the Union market, the later shop transaction does not restart the clock. Conversely, inventory sitting in Türkiye awaiting its first EU supply calls for a different analysis. Identify the finished units, the parties, the rights transferred and the agreement under which the stock moved. Refer uncertain cases to counsel with the underlying correspondence. Avoid silently populating blank placement dates with warehouse arrival dates.

Find the businesses behind the brand

Article 8 identifies potential defendants. The Turkish manufacturer can face a claim. For a manufacturer established outside the Union, the importer and the manufacturer's authorised representative can also fall within the liability chain. A fulfilment service provider enters the picture under specified conditions if neither an EU importer nor an EU authorised representative exists. A defective component can expose its own producer as well as the finished product manufacturer. Draw the actual supply chain before asking whose insurance responds.

Take a retail group that commissions an exclusive kettle bearing its own trademark. Its purchasing office negotiates in Istanbul, a German affiliate imports the goods and a separate company sells them online. A business presenting itself as manufacturer through its name or mark can be treated as one. Record the legal entities printed on the appliance and carton, the mandate given to any representative and the identity of the seller shown at checkout. Group branding obscures these distinctions precisely when a claimant needs an answer quickly.

A retailer receiving an identification request should route it to a named employee. Article 8 provides a conditional route to distributor liability where an EU operator covered by paragraph one cannot be identified and the distributor fails to identify an eligible operator or its own supplier within one month of the injured person's request. Preserve the original message, delivery timestamp, substantive response and supporting address. An automatic acknowledgement gives the customer a ticket number. It does little to identify the business that actually supplied her kettle.

Separate personal harm from the cost of a bad batch

The revised framework concerns harm suffered by natural persons. Article 6 covers death or personal injury, including medically recognised psychological harm. It also addresses specified property damage and the destruction or corruption of data used outside professional purposes. There are exclusions. The defective product itself and property used exclusively for professional purposes are among them. Separate each alleged loss before anyone drafts a settlement offer or accepts a supplier's estimate of the claim.

In the pouring incident, the burned hand, damaged kitchen flooring and replacement kettle belong on distinct lines. If the incident happened in a café, the injured employee and the café owner have different interests. The café's interrupted trading, replacement machinery and spoiled orders require analysis of other available legal routes and the supply contract. Document who owned the damaged property and how it was used. A photograph of a workspace rarely establishes whether an item served private needs, business needs or both.

Preserve a returned unit without disturbing the evidence

Ask customer service to record what the user was doing, roughly how full the vessel was and what happened immediately before and after the handle separated. Write down the speaker's words. Distinguish recollection from measurements. Arrange a safe collection and offer a contact for urgent assistance. Do not ask an injured customer to repeat the incident. Record consent and obtain only the personal details necessary to handle the report.

On receipt, photograph all sides, the base, power lead, label and detached pieces. Give the sealed package an identifier that survives transfers between the returns desk, independent laboratory and insurer. Keep original image files alongside any annotated copies. A loose screw found at the bottom of a box may have been inserted during packing or dislodged during transit. Describe where someone found it. A conclusion about its origin needs more evidence than its presence.

Before destructive examination, agree who will attend, how samples will be divided and what will remain available for another expert. Record storage temperature if it could affect a polymer fracture. Retain an untested comparator from a traceable batch if one exists. Ask the factory for the mould cavity number, resin specification, tightening instructions, operator training record and engineering changes relevant to the handle. Concentrate the request on the suspected mechanism. A generic demand for every factory record can delay the very evidence most likely to resolve the dispute.

When the product includes software

A connected appliance adds another question. Which version controlled this unit at the relevant moment? The Directive expressly includes software. Article 7 makes the safety assessment depend on the circumstances, including foreseeable use, interaction with other products and continuing manufacturer control. Article 11 qualifies certain defences when a defect arises from matters within that control, including some software updates or the absence of updates needed to maintain safety. These provisions matter even if the physical parts shipped months earlier.

Imagine an electric heater whose companion application changes the timing of a shutdown command. The component supplier delivered a working thermostat. The brand later distributed revised firmware. Photographing the broken appliance records only part of its history. Preserve release notes, the intended hardware range, installation logs where legitimately available, and the means of determining which version reached the individual unit. Distinguish a release offered to customers from one actually installed. Seek an engineer who can explain whether the device retained an independent thermal cutoff.

Construct a short chronology for the insurer. A serial number identifies the heater, an account record may indicate an update offer, and a service visit documents a replaced controller. Note gaps explicitly. A common dashboard displaying the latest release across an entire fleet can mislead someone examining an older event. Have the software provider explain what its historical fields capture, how long relevant records remain accessible and who can export readable copies when a contract ends.

Prepare for a focused disclosure request

Article 9 gives national courts a mechanism to order disclosure of relevant evidence where the claimant supports the plausibility of the claim. Defendants may also seek evidence under the applicable conditions. Necessity, proportionality and protection for confidential information matter. Article 10 retains a role for proof of defect, damage and causation but provides rebuttable presumptions in specified situations. Failure to comply with a disclosure obligation can affect the assessment of defectiveness.

If design tolerances are commercially sensitive, identify the precise drawings, revisions and passages at issue. Discuss protected access, limited recipients or redactions with local counsel. Preserve an intact master. An indiscriminate refusal can have procedural consequences, while voluntarily forwarding an entire supplier archive can expose unrelated customer designs. Prepare a plain English glossary for technical abbreviations. A judge studying a fracture report needs to understand what the test fixture measures and which failure modes fall outside its range.

Check whether the insurance programme follows the goods

Ask the broker to walk through one hypothetical accident using the actual schedule and endorsements. Identify the named insured, covered territories, deductible, reporting channel and any separate arrangements for recall expenditure. A policy summary retained in procurement may omit a condition affecting reimbursement. Compare the latest renewal with the period when the units entered distribution. If a customer service contractor receives the initial complaint on a Saturday, identify who can notify the insurer and arrange secure storage over the weekend. Record the broker's answer with the policy reference so the night shift can find it.

Put cooperation into the next purchase order

Use the supply agreement to decide who receives notices, arranges collection, commissions testing and authorises emergency spending. Address translation, reasonable access to component suppliers, retention of drawings and cooperation after termination. Specify how the importer can reach a responsible deputy during holidays in either country. An indemnity allocates costs between contracting parties. Article 15 prevents contractual terms from limiting or excluding the Directive liability of an economic operator towards the injured person.

Run a short exercise before the December deadline. Give a colleague a mock serial number, a retailer's complaint and a photograph with the rating plate partly obscured. Ask for the first supply record, component revision, user instructions and current insurance contact. Time the retrieval. If two suppliers used the same sales code, add a distinguishing batch reference. If the only person able to read test results has left the factory, commission a translation and identify a replacement technical witness.

Finish the exercise with a decision that the business can audit. For each open issue, record an owner, a deadline and the document that would resolve it. A buyer may keep purchasing from a capable manufacturer while withholding release of a particular disputed batch. An importer may arrange independent examination while preserving contractual notice periods. The common requirement is that someone can explain the decision using facts about the individual product, its route to market and the person reporting harm.

Frequently asked questions

Does the new regime cover a kettle first supplied in the EU on 9 December 2026?

Yes. The corrected boundary starts on 9 December. Establish first placement or putting into service and check the relevant national implementing law.

Can an importer pass all exposure to the factory by contract?

An indemnity can allocate costs between the parties. It cannot remove statutory rights held by an injured person.
