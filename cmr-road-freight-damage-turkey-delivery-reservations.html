Damaged Road Freight on a Türkiye Route: A CMR Guide for Receivers

International Trade & Commercial Disputes November 19, 2026 9 min read

A machine arrives at a German warehouse after a road journey from Bursa. One crate leans against the trailer wall. The driver needs a signature, the unloading team wants the bay clear and the purchasing manager is asking whether production can start on Monday. A short, accurate observation made while everyone is still present can be more valuable than a polished complaint written a week later. The receiver needs a way to record what is visible, protect the goods and notify the right party without guessing at the cause.

This guide follows a delivery from the gate to the claim file. It draws on the Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road, generally known as CMR. Its examples are practical suggestions for businesses shipping to or from Türkiye. The sale, the carriage contract and the cargo insurance policy each answer different questions. Keep the documents for all three available before deciding who should demand payment from whom.

Identify the carriage contract and the destination

Article 1 generally covers carriage of goods by road for reward when the place of taking over and the place designated for delivery are in different countries and at least one is a contracting country. Türkiye is a party. Check the agreed places, the nature of the carriage and any exceptions. Nationality of the driver, registration of the tractor and the language printed on the invoice do not settle the question. For a journey involving a ferry, rail segment or separate carriers, obtain route information before offering a definitive view of the applicable regime.

Ask who undertook the carriage. A freight forwarder may arrange a movement, perform it as carrier or assume different obligations under its booking. The name on a vehicle may belong to a subcontractor. Keep the booking confirmation, transport order, names and addresses, correspondence and consignment note. Article 4 recognises that an absent, irregular or lost consignment note does not by itself invalidate the contract. Other records can therefore matter considerably when a dispatch office cannot find the signed original.

Prepare receiving staff before a vehicle arrives

Give the shift supervisor a brief inspection sheet and the carrier's contact details. List the expected package count, serial numbers, packaging description and any temperature or handling specification. Provide a safe area where questionable goods can be held. Agree who may sign the delivery receipt and who can contact the insurer or appoint a surveyor. If unloading takes place outside office hours, provide an escalation number that someone actually monitors.

The warehouse can prepare a photo sequence in advance. Start with a wide view identifying the load and its position. Take a medium view showing the packaging and a close view of the damaged area beside a ruler or another meaningful scale. Keep original files with their timestamps. A cropped image of a scratch without a serial number, location or surrounding context may be difficult to match to a particular delivery. Photograph relevant labels while restricting access to unnecessary personal information.

Make the reservation describe what people can see

For apparent loss or damage, Article 30 addresses reservations to the carrier by delivery. Record a general indication of the observed problem. For example, “Crate three of eight, right lower corner crushed, two securing straps detached, inspection of contents pending” tells a subsequent reader much more than “subject to inspection.” Enter the observation on the delivery record, retain a copy and invite the driver to participate. For an electronic receipt, check whether both parties receive a legible version with the observation intact.

Do not assert that a forklift struck the crate unless someone witnessed it or other evidence supports it. Record whether unloading had begun, whether the trailer seal was intact and who saw the position of the crate. If a driver declines to sign, note the refusal, identify any witnesses and send the observations promptly to the carrier by a traceable channel. Signing a clean receipt can create an evidential problem. Under Article 30(1), unqualified receipt in the circumstances described is initial evidence of delivery as recorded. Its precise effect warrants examination on the facts.

Handle concealed damage and delay separately

A wooden case can look sound while a calibrated spindle inside has shifted. If loss or damage was not apparent at delivery, Article 30(1) calls for written reservations within seven days of delivery, excluding Sundays and public holidays. The date of delivery is excluded when calculating this period. Where the consignee and carrier jointly checked the condition, Article 30(2) provides a specific evidential rule for non-apparent loss or damage, including written reservations within seven days of checking, with Sundays and public holidays excepted. Record the date and scope of any joint check and obtain local advice if calendars differ.

Delay follows a separate rule. Article 30(3) requires a written reservation to the carrier within twenty-one days from the time the goods were placed at the consignee's disposal if compensation for delay is sought. The triggering day is excluded. Do not assume the seven-day exclusions apply to that separate period. Note the agreed delivery window, appointment confirmations, gate entry, time actually made available and the resulting disruption. An internal helpdesk ticket or a message sent solely to the goods seller may never reach the carrier.

Assemble a notice that can be matched to the shipment

Identify the sender of the notice, the contractual carrier and the person receiving it. Include the transport reference, collection and delivery locations, relevant dates, vehicle identification where known, affected packages and a factual description. Invite inspection and provide contact details and practical access arrangements. Where a dismantling decision is pending, explain what can be seen without removing protective material. State what further examination is planned. Keep the message, attachments and proof of transmission or delivery together.

Imagine two identical pumps delivered on consecutive mornings. An email saying “your pump is damaged” leaves open which load, item and incident it concerns. A serial number, consignment reference and dated photographs resolve that ambiguity. Send a supplementary report when the repair technician diagnoses the defect. Preserve the earlier notice. Replacing it with an updated PDF can obscure what the carrier was told, when it was told and whether there was a fair opportunity to inspect.

Preserve evidence without worsening the loss

Isolate a wet pallet from dry stock. Move vulnerable electronics out of standing water if it is safe to do so. A record of urgent protective action can explain why the packaging looked different when the surveyor arrived. Log who authorised the move, its timing and its purpose. Keep wrapping, seals, desiccant packs and shock indicators if relevant. Ask an engineer before powering up equipment that may have suffered impact or moisture exposure. A test performed in haste can compromise both safety and diagnosis.

Arrange a joint survey where appropriate. Invite the relevant parties with reasonable notice, provide directions and identify any safety or access conditions. Ask the surveyor to distinguish observations from inferences and to explain any limitation caused by delayed access. A technician may identify a fractured mount but be unable to date the fracture. A packaging specialist may need pre-dispatch photographs to assess cushioning. Retain dissenting observations rather than editing an inspection minute until it appears everyone agreed.

Build a defensible valuation

A replacement invoice is only one item in a valuation file. Compare the goods as dispatched with their condition at destination. Collect purchase documents, packing lists, commercial invoices, weights, repair estimates and any sale or disposal proceeds. Ask whether repair restores performance and warranty, whether an unaffected part remains usable, and whether a resale market exists. Record the reasons for a scrap decision and obtain disposal documentation where appropriate. Under Articles 23 and 25, compensation calculations and limits require attention to the type and extent of loss. Check applicable protocols and the circumstances of the route before quoting a monetary ceiling.

A factory may pay overtime, hire a substitute machine or face a contractual penalty downstream. Preserve evidence of these consequences in a separate schedule. Proof that a business incurred an expense does not alone establish its recoverability against a carrier. Identify the legal basis, causal link and any applicable limit before presenting a total. If one invoice combines repairs with an unrelated upgrade, ask the vendor for a breakdown. Keep currency, tax treatment, freight and salvage proceeds visible so that different versions of the claim can be reconciled.

Deal with a disagreement about packing

Suppose a carrier points to insufficient bracing while the exporter says the truck braked suddenly. Preserve both accounts. Request the packing specification, assembly photographs, fastener dimensions, loading plan and any contemporaneous reservations at collection. Article 8 addresses checks at takeover, including package count, markings and apparent condition, together with reservations where appropriate. Article 9 explains the evidential role of the consignment note. A clean exterior can conceal an internal defect. Keep what was visible to the driver distinct from what remained inside a sealed crate.

If a specialist examines vibration data, ask for the recorder model, calibration history, mounting position and original electronic file. An alarm above a preset threshold may justify investigation while leaving the time, mechanism and severity of the damage unresolved. Request permission before destructive testing. Agree who will retain removed parts, who may attend the examination and where the signed laboratory record will be stored. These arrangements can preserve a meaningful opportunity for all interested parties to assess competing explanations.

Coordinate the buyer, seller, insurer and carrier

A delivery term in a sales contract may allocate risk between buyer and seller. It does not establish when the physical damage occurred or who may recover under the carriage contract. Review the contract wording and the named place. Record title, risk, possession, insurance and entitlement to bring a claim as separate questions. Obtain any authority needed for a logistics manager or survey agent to act. A consignee may hold evidence that another party ultimately needs to pursue recovery.

Notify the cargo insurer or broker in accordance with the actual policy. Check survey instructions and consent requirements before disposal, settlement or a broad release. Where an insurer pays, coordinate any recovery rights and avoid competing demands for the same loss. A goodwill credit from the seller should be recorded with its terms. A commercial compromise between trading partners may leave unresolved questions about ownership of salvaged equipment or who may continue proceedings against the transport provider.

Keep a separate calendar for legal proceedings

Article 32 provides a general one-year limitation period and a three-year period in specified misconduct cases. The starting point varies with partial loss, damage, delay, total loss and other cases. A written claim can suspend limitation under the article's conditions until written rejection and return of attached documents. Obtain advice about when a communication qualifies, proof of receipt and the effect of any reply. A reservation at delivery, an insurer notification, a monetary demand and the commencement of proceedings deserve separate entries in the file.

Close the shift with a short handover. The receiving supervisor identifies the goods and observations. Claims staff confirm that the carrier has received the reservation. The technical lead sets an inspection date. Finance opens a loss schedule, and counsel records the applicable deadlines. When a colleague opens the folder the following morning, they should be able to locate the original receipt, read the first notice and find the person who controls the goods. That clarity gives the business room to negotiate while the evidence is still available.

Frequently asked questions

What should a receiver record when a crate is visibly damaged?

Identify the package and the damage visible at delivery, document the observation with the carrier, retain a copy and invite inspection. Photograph the packaging before it changes.

Does a notice to the insurer also notify the carrier?

Check who actually received the communication. Send the appropriate reservation to the carrier and notify the insurer under the policy. Preserve transmission records for both.
