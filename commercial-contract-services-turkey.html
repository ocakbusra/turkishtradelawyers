Commercial Contracts in Turkey — Key Legal Points

Turkish Trade Lawyers advises foreign and domestic businesses on commercial contract drafting, review, negotiation and localisation in Turkey. The Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 provides the principal general contract framework, while the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 adds rules for merchants and specific commercial relationships.

In cross-border transactions, Article 24 of Law No. 5718 generally recognises party choice of governing law, subject to mandatory rules and public policy. Law No. 805 may require Turkish-language use in specified commercial contexts, and the CISG may apply to qualifying international sales involving Türkiye.

Localising Global Contracts for Turkey

A global template provides consistency across a multinational group, but it should not be assumed to work in Turkey merely because the commercial deal is similar. We identify the Turkish legal layer that translation alone cannot solve.

Translation Is Not Localisation

Translation asks what a clause says in Turkish. Localisation asks what the clause does under Turkish law, how it will operate in practice and what evidence will be available if the relationship fails.

Distribution and Agency Agreements in Turkey

Distribution is a common route for foreign manufacturers and brands entering the Turkish market. A distributor normally buys and resells in its own name and for its own account; a commercial agent acts as an independent intermediary and is specifically regulated by the Turkish Commercial Code.

Distribution Agreements

We cover exclusive or non-exclusive appointment, territory, channels, online sales, marketplaces, key accounts, targets, forecasts, inventory, pricing, marketing, brand use, sub-distributors, import responsibility, customs, conformity, warranty, recalls, reporting, audit, competition, term, termination, sell-off and remaining inventory.

Commercial Agency

We advise on appointment, authority, territory, commission, customer ownership, reporting, expenses, exclusivity, competing products, sub-agents, confidentiality, notice, termination, post-termination obligations and non-compete terms.

Exit Exposure

Article 122 of the Turkish Commercial Code creates a potential goodwill or portfolio indemnity for qualifying agents. In appropriate cases, the framework may also apply to exclusive distributorships and similar continuing monopoly-type relationships.

Manufacturing, OEM and Private Label

Quality-Control Workflow

If payment depends on laboratory reports, inspection reports, production milestones or loading supervision, those documents should be expressly linked to payment and acceptance provisions.

International Sale of Goods, CISG and Contract Language

Türkiye has been a Contracting State to the CISG since 1 August 2011. The Convention may apply to qualifying international sales involving Turkish businesses unless excluded or otherwise inapplicable. The parties should decide deliberately whether to apply or exclude it.

International Sales

We review product description, quantity, price, payment, documentary requirements, delivery, risk, title, inspection, conformity, defects, notice, cure, replacement, damages, force majeure, hardship, avoidance and dispute resolution.

Incoterms Do Not Replace the Contract

An Incoterm allocates delivery-related cost, risk and responsibility. It does not replace provisions on title, payment, conformity, warranties, liability, regulatory compliance or remedies.

English and Bilingual Agreements

Law No. 805 creates Turkish-language requirements in specified contexts. We assess the parties, transaction, authority submissions, prevailing version, signing mechanics and evidence before recommending English-only or bilingual execution.

Payment Security, Guarantees and Liability Allocation

A contract with strong liability wording can still be commercially weak if payment is unsecured. The transaction structure should answer what happens if the counterparty performs badly, refuses to pay or becomes financially distressed.

Payment Security

We structure advance payments, deposits, milestones, documentary conditions, retention, deferred payment, credit periods, set-off, withholding, default interest, suspension rights and release conditions.

Security tools may include letters of credit, bank guarantees, demand guarantees, standby instruments, parent-company guarantees and security deposits.

Liability Architecture

We review contractual penalties, delay penalties, service credits, indemnities, direct and consequential loss, liability caps, carve-outs, warranties, third-party claims, product liability and insurance.

Penalty and Mandatory Rules

Common-law drafting concepts should not be copied into Turkish contracts without localisation. Turkish mandatory rules can restrict exclusions, especially where intentional or grossly negligent conduct is involved.

NDA, IP, Data and Competition Clauses

Commercial risk often begins before the definitive contract is signed. We prepare and review pre-contract documents and the compliance clauses that allocate regulatory and operational responsibility.

NDAs and Term Sheets

IP Ownership and Use Rights

Commercial contracts should distinguish background IP, project IP, licensed IP and improvements. We advise on ownership, licences, sublicensing, territory, field of use, know-how, software, work product, enforcement and post-termination use.

KVKK and Compliance

We review controller and processor roles, cross-border transfers, security, incident reporting, deletion, audit, regulatory cooperation, anti-bribery, sanctions, export controls, competition and sector-specific compliance.

Exclusivity and Non-Compete

Exclusive territories, channels, sole sourcing, minimum purchases, online restrictions and non-compete terms should be checked against commercial objective, duration, market position and Turkish competition rules.

Governing Law, Notices and Dispute Resolution

Article 24 of Law No. 5718 generally recognises the law selected by the parties within the applicable statutory framework. The workable choice depends on mandatory Turkish rules, public policy, asset location, counterparty location, enforcement, confidentiality, interim relief, speed and cost.

Governing Law

We assess Turkish law, foreign law selection, regulatory overlays, CISG interaction, dispute forum and enforcement location together rather than treating governing law as boilerplate.

Courts or Arbitration

We advise on Turkish courts, foreign courts, ISTAC, ICC or ad hoc arbitration, seat, language, tribunal, interim relief and enforcement strategy.

Commercial Mediation

Certain commercial receivable and compensation claims require mediation before court litigation. The clause and pre-dispute strategy should reflect the actual Turkish procedural path.

Notices and KEP

For merchants, Article 18/3 of the Turkish Commercial Code identifies notary, registered letter, telegram and registered electronic mail (KEP) using secure electronic signature for specified default, termination and withdrawal notices.

Force Majeure, Termination and Contract Breach

The exit clause should be negotiated before the relationship begins. A dispute should begin with a rights-and-evidence assessment, not an emotional demand letter.

Force Majeure and Hardship

Force majeure addresses events that prevent or materially disrupt performance. Hardship addresses circumstances where performance remains possible but the economic balance changes radically. We review notice, evidence, mitigation, suspension, renegotiation, price adjustment and long-stop dates.
