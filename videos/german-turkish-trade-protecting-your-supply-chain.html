Contractual and legal options when delivery or quality problems disrupt a German–Turkish supply chain.

Original video description

Managing cross-border supply chains between Germany and Türkiye requires immediate legal risk management. When unexpected delivery defaults or quality issues arise in automotive, machinery, or textile corridors, foreign corporations have powerful local remedies available. Under Turkish commercial law, an "İhtiyati Haciz" (preliminary attachment) allows companies to secure their claims by freezing a local supplier’s physical stock and bank accounts rapidly—without traditional delays. Furthermore, bilateral treaties heavily favor German entities by fully exempting them from posting financial security deposits (teminat muafiyeti) when initiating legal proceedings in Turkish courts. From securing international data flows under KVKK compliance (VERBIS registration) to executing corporate actions remotely via a German notary and Landgericht Apostille, managing your Turkish operations does not require physical travel. Understanding these legal frameworks ensures continuous corporate compliance and asset protection.
