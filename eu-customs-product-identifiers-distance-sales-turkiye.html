Which Product Identifiers Do EU Customs Require for Online Orders from Türkiye?

International Trade & Customs November 24, 2026 9 min read

A French customer orders a blue bath towel from a marketplace. A warehouse outside Istanbul has the right size, colour and delivery address. Yet the courier asks for three product references before it will collect the parcel. The seller sends the order confirmation. It identifies a transaction involving that customer, but tells the declarant little about which towel was offered, who made it or how another retailer might describe the same model. Start with the listing, the factory specification and the retail packaging. Each answers a different part of the courier's question.

For businesses dispatching goods from Türkiye directly to EU customers, the change became mandatory on 1 November 2026. The European Commission explained it in a notice published on 3 November. Under Delegated Regulation 2026/1022, operators could supply the new particulars voluntarily from 1 July. A merchant reading the November announcement after launch should therefore review orders already dispatched since the start of the month as well as tomorrow's outgoing consignments. Agree with the customs representative how to handle a discrepancy in a declaration already lodged.

Which Turkish sales route needs this information?

Draw the route of one actual order. Where were the goods when the customer bought them? Who arranged transport? Which business booked the sale, controlled the online offer and handed the package to the carrier? A homeware brand accepting an order in France for delivery from its Turkish fulfilment partner has a different transaction from a wholesaler moving unsold stock into its own European distribution centre. Later domestic delivery from that stock has a different customs history. A Turkish seller address alone cannot settle the treatment.

Identify qualifying distance sales of imported goods with the declarant. Tell them if the order exceeded EUR 150, if preferential treatment is being claimed or if the consignment needs a full declaration. The simplified low-value declaration, the amount of duty and the obligation to identify goods answer separate questions. A commercial invoice or an A.TR movement certificate may serve a purpose in the same shipment. Neither supplies the three references by itself. Commission guidance for customs and trade explains how the identifiers fit different declaration routes and describes specific administrative tolerances.

Read the codes from the offer back to the maker

Begin with the merchant product identifier, or M-PID. On a marketplace, ask for the platform's reference for the product offered there. A seller's internal inventory number can look convincingly similar. Open the original offer and check whether the reference supplied by the platform actually leads to that product. On a merchant's own shop the relevant identifier may come from its own catalogue. A purchase number changes when another shopper checks out. The identifier for the unchanged offer should still point to the same merchandise.

Next obtain the non-standardised manufacturer product identifier, or NS-PID. For the towel it might be the mill's style and colour reference as used on the order sheet or wrapper. The manufacturer can explain whether a suffix identifies size, finish, weaving pattern or simply an internal production shift. The aim is to trace the model across sellers. A warehouse bin number points to a shelf whose occupant can change next week. Keep bin and model in separate fields even when the warehouse staff use both to find an item.

The standardised manufacturer product identifier, or S-PID, follows a recognised identification system. A retail barcode may encode an EAN or UPC. A printed barcode can equally encode the warehouse's private reference. Scan it, establish the numbering system and compare the result with the manufacturer's particulars for the exact unit. If no standardised identifier exists, the declarant has a specific absence code. A blank cell because nobody has checked is an unresolved question. Ask the producer or brand owner to confirm the position.

Follow one towel through the declaration

Consider an illustrative order for one blue bath towel. Its platform reference is BEACHBLUE80. The mill uses DB80-NAVY for this size and finish. The consumer sleeve carries an assigned international trade item number. The merchant exports the platform reference beside the selected variant, the mill confirms its own style, and the packing team scans the sleeve around the single towel. The customs representative receives the three values linked to this order line, together with the source of each. Someone opening a carton of six units would see a different packaging level.

In the Commission's technical guidance, C127 carries the merchant reference, C128 the manufacturer's own reference and C129 the standardised identifier. Those references belong in the supporting-document data. When the individual product genuinely lacks a standardised identifier, Y081 records that fact in the additional-reference field. Ask the broker to show a sample output with the codes alongside their values. An interface labelled simply “barcode” can conceal a mapping error until someone reads the declaration that actually leaves the system.

On a second platform the same towel may have an entirely different merchant reference. Preserve the original reference with the selling channel, account and listing version used in that particular transaction. Do not overwrite the first marketplace's record when adding the second. Occasionally two identifier types genuinely share the same value. The guidance allows this in specified circumstances and still calls for each relevant document code. Copying a convenient value into every field as a universal default destroys the very distinction the declaration relies on.

Handle gift sets and products sold together

Suppose the customer also buys a matching hand towel. Both sizes travel in one postal bag. Even if the broker lawfully groups multiple products on one customs item, the applicable identifiers for each product still need to be reported. The supporting-document field can repeat. Ask for an export that shows every repetition. A dispatch application which stores only one value against the bag will silently drop the second product's identity. Compare the correspondence between order lines, packed units and declaration items before releasing such orders at scale.

A seasonal gift set poses a further question. The box contains a towel with an international identifier and a matching accessory without one. Ask which products are grouped under each declaration line. For grouped products the guidance calls for the existing standardised identifiers to be reported. The absence code applies when none of the grouped products has one. Preserve a component list and a photograph of the retail presentation. They let the broker understand what was sold together and later explain why only one standardised number appears on that line.

Make exceptions visible before carrier collection

At a Turkish packing station, one simple test catches surprisingly expensive errors. Scan the smallest retail unit and the outer case in succession. Display the decoded strings in full, including leading zeroes. Ask the operator to select the level that matches what the shopper bought. Spreadsheet software can strip a zero, convert a long number into scientific notation or silently turn a style into a date. Import these columns as text and compare the returned values character for character after transferring them to the carrier.

An apparently minor packaging substitution deserves attention. A supplier runs out of navy sleeves and temporarily wraps blue towels in plain paper. Picking staff can still recognise the goods. The retailer's product record may still contain the correct recognised identifier. Retrieve the underlying assignment and a previous approved sleeve, then confirm with the mill that the batch contains the same model. Record who verified it. Missing ink alone does not show that the product lacks an assigned standardised number.

If a brand recently switched factories, ask whether the incoming producer uses the outgoing mill's model designation under licence or assigns its own. Check a purchase order, a sewn label and a dated specification from the new source. Distinguish a genuine continuation from a similar-looking replacement. The merchandising department can keep its commercial description “Aegean bath towel” while the customs data team records a different manufacturing reference for newly received inventory. Quarantine only the uncertain combination if other lots remain independently identifiable.

Agree who can correct the record

For a British retailer using a Turkish fulfilment house to supply EU consumers, four teams may maintain four separate systems. Merchandising owns the offer. Procurement deals with the mill. A parcel carrier transmits data. A customs representative lodges the declaration. Give each a named contact and a short response window for mismatches. Confirm who can pause dispatch, who supplies replacement evidence and who authorises release. A logistics contract can allocate these tasks and extra storage costs without changing the declarant's obligations towards customs.

Run a small acceptance exercise before changing the live integration. Include one sale on your own website, one marketplace sale, two variants sharing a base style, a legitimate absence of a standardised number and a mixed order. Export the platform file, send it through the normal warehouse connector and inspect the broker's resulting fields. Compare expected and observed output. Preserve the original messages and corrected mapping. If the carrier cannot return an example containing repeated fields, resolve that limitation before giving it a large consolidated dispatch.

Trace a query back to the particular sale

Imagine customs asks about stock delivered three weeks earlier. The live web page now features a winter replacement, the fulfilment contractor has moved bins, and the previous buyer has left the company. A small transaction record can still answer the question. Keep the date, channel, purchased variant, supplier reference, standardised number or documented absence, dispatch identifier and broker acknowledgment. Link to a dated offer capture and the version of the product sheet used for that order. Limit unnecessary customer details in copies circulated between commercial teams.

During a seasonal promotion, a buyer may substitute a larger towel at the same price when the original size sells out. Customer consent to the upgrade belongs in the order history. The changed size must also reach the packing instruction and the declarant's data. Reconcile the amended line against the item physically leaving the premises. If the declaration has already been submitted, ask the representative about the appropriate correction and retain its reply with the shipment. Editing the storefront will only affect future shoppers.

Check what happens when the weekend shift takes over. Leave a sealed demonstration parcel, a printed pick ticket and access to the training environment. Invite a supervisor who did not configure the interface to reconstruct the transmitted declaration. Note any step that depends on remembering an abbreviation, opening a personal mailbox or telephoning somebody on leave. Replace those dependencies with a shared reference and a documented escalation contact. Repeat the exercise after a platform migration or warehouse relocation.

Measure the problem in units your operations director can act on. Count held parcels by cause, corrections after submission, suppliers awaiting confirmation and repeated failures by connector. Check a sample of successful orders too. A message accepted by software can still contain the wrong product reference. A short review using an actual photographed parcel, an archived listing and the corresponding customs output shows whether the chain works. When each participant can find the same towel from their own records, the next query has an answer before the afternoon collection leaves.

Frequently asked questions

What if the manufacturer never assigned a standardised identifier?

For a single product, the declarant reports Y081 for the absence of that identifier. Establish the facts with the manufacturer and still provide the merchant and manufacturer references.

Can a marketplace order number identify the goods?

An order number identifies a transaction. Retrieve the product reference for the particular offer and obtain the applicable manufacturer identifiers.
