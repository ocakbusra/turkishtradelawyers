Enforcing Foreign Arbitral Awards in Turkey

Recognition and enforcement in Türkiye: legal routes, required documents and practical steps

January 14, 2026 8 min read Updated: September 25, 2026

A foreign arbitral award is not automatically executable against assets in Türkiye. A creditor generally needs a Turkish court decision recognising or enforcing the award before taking local execution steps. The applicable treaty, the award’s status, the parties’ agreement, the documents and the debtor’s location all need to be checked before filing.

This guide explains the legal questions a foreign award creditor should resolve first. It does not predict a court outcome or replace advice on a particular award, arbitration agreement or asset position.

1. Identify the Applicable Legal Route

For commercial awards made abroad, the first instrument to check is the 1958 New York Convention. The United Nations treaty record lists Türkiye’s declaration: the Convention is applied on a reciprocity basis to awards made in another contracting State and to disputes arising from legal relationships considered commercial under Turkish law.

Where the Convention applies, its recognition and enforcement rules operate alongside Turkish procedure. Turkish International Private and Procedural Law No. 5718 (MÖHUK) also sets out a domestic route for foreign arbitral awards in Articles 60–62. Counsel should determine which instrument governs the specific award, including the seat, the State where it was made, the commercial nature of the dispute and any other applicable treaty or statutory rule.

2. Confirm the Award’s Status and Scope

Check the operative part of the award, its date, the place of arbitration, the arbitration clause and any later correction or interpretation decision. Under MÖHUK Article 60, a foreign award may be enforced if it is final and has become enforceable or binding on the parties. The Convention also permits refusal where the award is not yet binding, has been set aside or suspended by a competent authority at the seat, subject to the Convention’s terms.

Confirm whether the award grants a payment, orders another act or allocates costs; identify the legal entity against which relief is granted; and check whether any part of the award falls outside the arbitration agreement. A mismatch between the named debtor, the contracting party and the entity holding assets can affect the execution plan.

3. Select the Court and Venue

MÖHUK Article 60 directs an enforcement application to the civil court of first instance. If the parties have agreed in writing on a Turkish court, the application is made there. Without such an agreement, the statutory venue is generally the court for the opposing party’s domicile in Türkiye; if it has no domicile, its place of residence; and if neither is available, the place where assets that may be subject to execution are located.

Before filing, verify the correct debtor, available corporate and service details, the court connected to the chosen venue and whether the arbitration agreement or the applicable instrument affects the procedural route. Do not select a court solely because the arbitration institution or hearing venue was in that city.

4. Prepare the Award and Supporting Documents

MÖHUK Article 61 requires the enforcement request to be accompanied by the arbitration agreement or clause and the award in original or duly approved copies, together with duly approved translations. The New York Convention Article IV also specifies the award and arbitration agreement and a certified translation where the documents are not in an official language of the enforcement country. The precise authentication and translation route should be checked for the document’s issuing country and the instrument being relied on.

  • Original or properly authenticated/certified copy of the complete award, including any correction or interpretation decision.
  • The arbitration agreement or the contract containing the arbitration clause, in the required original or certified form.
  • Evidence that the award is binding or enforceable where required by the applicable route, and information about any set-aside or suspension proceedings.
  • Turkish translations prepared and certified in the form required for the filing.
  • Copies for the opposing parties and documents establishing party identity, representation and the correct respondent.

An apostille is not a substitute for checking every authentication rule, and it should not be assumed to be required or sufficient in every case. Verify the applicable treaty, local filing practice and certification route before arranging originals.

5. Understand the Grounds on Which Enforcement May Be Refused

The New York Convention Article V and MÖHUK Article 62 set out grounds that may prevent enforcement. Depending on the applicable route and evidence, these include:

  • The arbitration agreement was invalid or a party lacked capacity.
  • The opposing party did not receive proper notice of the arbitrator’s appointment or proceedings, or could not present its case.
  • The award decides matters outside the submission to arbitration; a separable portion may be treated differently.
  • The tribunal’s composition or procedure did not follow the parties’ agreement or the applicable law.
  • The award is not binding, has been set aside or suspended by a competent authority where the applicable instrument permits that objection.
  • The subject matter is not capable of arbitration under Turkish law or enforcement would violate Turkish public policy.

These are enforcement-stage objections, not a general appeal on the merits. The Turkish court does not simply retry the underlying commercial dispute because a party disagrees with the tribunal’s factual or legal conclusions. The specific documents and argument still matter, and public policy and arbitrability issues are assessed under the applicable legal rules.

6. Consider Whether Only Part of the Award Is Contested

Where an award includes decisions on matters both within and outside the arbitration submission, the Convention and MÖHUK contemplate that a separable part may be treated independently. Identify the precise paragraphs or amounts in dispute and explain whether the remainder can operate on its own. Do not assume that a challenge to one part automatically defeats the entire award.

7. Plan for Execution After the Court Decision

Recognition or enforcement is a court stage; it is not the same as receiving payment. After obtaining the required decision, the creditor may need separate execution steps against the debtor’s assets in Türkiye. Map the correct debtor, asset ownership, location, encumbrances and available enforcement route before filing so that the court process connects to a practical recovery plan.

Where asset dissipation is a concern, ask Turkish counsel whether any interim protective measure is available on the facts and what evidence, security or separate application it may require. An award creditor should not assume that a protective measure follows automatically from an arbitration award or an enforcement application.

8. Pre-Filing Checklist for Foreign Award Creditors

Information to assemble

  • Seat and place of the arbitration, date of the award, administering institution and any subsequent award decisions.
  • Arbitration agreement, governing law, parties and exact legal name of the award debtor.
  • Evidence of finality, binding effect, enforceability, service and participation in the arbitration.
  • Any set-aside, suspension, annulment or challenge proceedings and their current status.
  • Originals, certification, translations and number of copies required for the chosen route.
  • Evidence of the debtor’s Turkish domicile, residence or assets relevant to venue and execution.
  • Potential objections, separable portions of the award and steps needed to preserve recovery options.

There is no reliable universal timeline or cost estimate for every case. Service, document collection, objections, court workload, appeals, translations and separate execution steps can all affect duration and budget. Obtain a current case-specific estimate after reviewing the award and supporting file.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are foreign arbitral awards automatically enforceable in Türkiye?

No. A creditor generally needs the relevant Turkish recognition or enforcement decision before taking execution steps against assets in Türkiye.

Will the Turkish court reconsider the commercial merits?

The enforcement review is limited by the applicable treaty and Turkish law. It is not a general rehearing of the dispute, although recognised refusal grounds must still be assessed.

Does every foreign award fall under the New York Convention?

No. Check the place where the award was made, the Convention’s contracting-State and commercial reservations, and whether another treaty or the Turkish domestic statute applies.

Is an apostille always required?

Do not assume so. The required authentication depends on the document, country of origin, applicable convention and Turkish filing requirements. Confirm the route before obtaining or translating originals.

What happens after the enforcement order?

The creditor may still need separate execution proceedings and asset-specific steps. The order does not itself guarantee payment or identify assets.
