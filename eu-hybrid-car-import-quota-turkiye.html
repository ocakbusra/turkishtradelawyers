EU Quotas for Hybrid Cars from Türkiye: Can Your December Shipment Clear?

International Trade & Customs November 26, 2026 9 min read

A dealer in Rotterdam has promised a fleet customer sixty new hybrids assembled in Türkiye. The factory has allocated the cars, the vessel has space and the buyer has arranged finance. From 1 December 2026, those arrangements leave one question open. Is there enough EU quota for the importer to clear the vehicles? If the relevant allocation runs out, paying an extra duty will not secure entry. That distinction belongs in the delivery promise before anyone commits to a handover date.

The European Commission adopted Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2614 on 23 November and announced the provisional safeguard on 24 November, when the text appeared in the Official Journal. Article 7 sets 1 December as the date it enters into force. Annex II gives Turkish-origin vehicles their own allocation. A purchasing director reviewing December deliveries therefore has a specific country row, product definition and timetable to test against the order book.

Start with the propulsion system

Take one vehicle specification and ask the manufacturer what actually drives the wheels. The measure covers certain new passenger vehicles designed for a maximum of nine occupants including the driver. They combine a combustion piston engine with at least one electric motor used for propulsion. External charging is optional. A plug-in family car can qualify, as can a mild hybrid whose electrical system assists the engine in moving the vehicle. Engine displacement or the colour of a charging socket will not settle the question.

A starter that simply restarts a petrol engine at traffic lights illustrates the boundary. If the electrical equipment only operates non-propulsion functions, the car falls outside the quota description. Some vehicles outside that description appear in Annex III for retrospective surveillance. The regulation treats these as distinct arrangements. Give the broker the technical sheet, the passenger capacity, the condition on import and the proposed ten-digit tariff classification. Ask for the reasoning behind any discrepancy between the factory code and the code used at destination.

Separate trim packages that carry the same model name. A manufacturer might offer a conventional engine, an assisted drive and an externally rechargeable powertrain under one badge. Photographing the boot emblem proves little about the variant on the transporter. Match the vehicle identification number to the build sheet and obtain an engineering contact who can answer a question about torque assistance. Maintain the approved classification with the specification revision so future orders inherit the reasoning only while the hardware remains the same.

Establish which country allocation applies

Article 4 uses non-preferential origin. A vessel sailing from Gemlik identifies a port of dispatch. A Turkish sales invoice identifies the contracting seller. Neither fact alone answers where a vehicle originates under EU customs law. An A.TR movement certificate can support the separate question of free circulation under the customs union. A buyer who relies on it should still keep the underlying origin assessment for the safeguard.

Compare two purchase orders. The first concerns cars manufactured at an established Turkish production plant. Ask the maker to identify the plant, describe the operations and support its conclusion on origin. The second concerns finished imports kept in a Turkish distribution yard before onward sale. The parties should investigate the manufacturing country before choosing a quota number. Repacking accessories, changing a sales intermediary or departing from a different terminal does not establish Turkish origin. A contested conclusion merits specialist customs advice before shipment.

For an assembly venture using parts sourced worldwide, draw a process map. Identify where bodies are formed, components installed and finished vehicles tested. Attach the relevant supplier declarations and cost evidence to the analysis where needed. Keep commercially sensitive supporting material accessible through an agreed confidential channel. The importer should know who will explain the production record to customs if the seller, factory and parent company operate in different time zones.

Put the three periods into the delivery calendar

Annex II identifies the Turkish allocation under order number 090256. The opening volume is 56,844 vehicles for 1 December 2026 through 28 February 2027. The second volume is 58,107 for 1 March through 31 May. The final, shorter period is 1 through 18 June, with 11,369 units. The declarant must claim the appropriate quota for covered imports. Within quota, the ordinary applicable duty treatment continues, including any relevant preference. The table measures individual vehicles. It provides a common pool for the origin across the Union. No dealer, port, brand or Turkish factory receives an individual entitlement from that table.

Create a calendar with a column for the estimated date on which customs will accept the relevant declaration. Beside it record vessel arrival, discharge slot, completion of inspection and expected delivery to the dealership. A sailing on 26 February could discharge on 2 March. A declaration can be delayed while an identification mismatch is corrected. A weekly logistics forecast that tracks only departure dates will put some vehicles in the wrong quarter.

Article 2 provides for unused volumes to carry into the next period within the same yearly period of application. Allocations for a quarter stop on the twentieth Commission working day after it ends. This administrative timetable allows outstanding drawings to be processed. It gives a buyer no basis for assuming that a fresh declaration accepted in March can draw on February simply because an older request remains unsettled. Ask the broker to show which period applies to the actual declaration.

Read the balance before dispatch and again before clearance

The Commission’s quota guidance explains how to consult current balances. For these safeguards, the customs authorities transmit valid requests and the Commission allocates quantities by reference to the acceptance dates of declarations. The implementing rules on quota management describe that sequence. Opening a purchase order, paying a deposit or obtaining a screenshot of a positive balance does not reserve vehicles. A second broker handling another shipment may be looking at the same balance.

Record the order number, observation time, balance, pending requests where displayed and the employee who obtained the information. Save the result with the shipment file. Repeat the check when booking the carrier and immediately before declaration. If the available quantity is falling quickly, shorten the interval between checks and tell sales which assumptions could move the customer’s collection date. Confirm actual allocation with the representative before releasing stock for unconditional delivery.

Article 2 also addresses competing requests. Suppose requests with equal priority total eighty vehicles and forty remain. In a simplified proportional allocation, an application for twenty would receive ten. With a smaller balance, fractional results are rounded down to whole vehicles. The quota closes on that allocation date. Article 2 deals separately with the rounded residue. Do not add residual fractions together on a dealer spreadsheet and promise an extra car.

Work through a mixed fleet order

A leasing company orders sixty cars. Thirty-eight are Turkish-origin new plug-in hybrids, twelve are new Turkish models whose electric motor assists propulsion, and ten are demonstrators with uncertain prior use. The first fifty go into the quota forecast, assuming classification confirms coverage. Send the demonstrator history for a separate new-or-used assessment. A description on a discounted invoice is weak evidence of condition. Registration, mileage, showroom use and individual records help the broker establish the relevant facts.

Assume the authority confirms allocation for thirty of the fifty covered units. The shortfall is twenty. Identify the individual vehicles that can move forward with customs, reconcile the quantities to the accepted documents and hold the balance under an arrangement approved by the responsible authorities. Check with the terminal beforehand about permitted storage, battery care, insurance, supervision and the timing of any movement. A commercial decision to delay delivery does not by itself authorize a customs procedure.

Now translate that shortfall into customer consequences. Two accessible vehicles may have appointments for specialist adaptations. Six taxis may need licensing inspections before an airport contract begins. Some private customers may already have sold their old vehicles. Agree a transparent allocation sequence with the distributor and obtain consent for permitted substitutions. Offering a different colour is easier than changing a powertrain that affects range, reimbursement or the connection needed at a fleet depot.

Divide the cost of waiting before signing

A fleet agreement often states who pays freight but says little about the cost of an unavailable quota. Identify the importer of record, the party instructing the representative and the person allowed to change a booking. Specify the evidence that triggers a revised delivery estimate. Decide how long the buyer can defer collection, which storage charges require approval, and when either party can cancel an undelivered balance. Address refunds and the treatment of deposits when part of a batch clears.

For a worked budget, assume an agreed yard tariff of eight euros per car per day and twenty vehicles waiting for twelve days. That produces EUR 1,920 before insurance, inspection, financing or onward transport. These figures are illustrative commercial assumptions. Replace them with written quotations for the relevant terminal. The cost of a missed demonstration or an idle conversion workshop may exceed the yard bill. Assign those exposures explicitly in the supply agreement if the parties expect compensation.

Request a written instruction for exceptions. A regional manager might authorise two additional storage days while a director must approve diversion to another commercial destination. Give the haulier a named decision maker and a deadline. Describe how an instruction reaches the consignee on a Saturday when the purchasing office is closed. A routing change can affect customs status, warranty coverage, insurance territory and the buyer’s right to reject late performance. Obtain the relevant confirmations before a driver moves a single vehicle.

Give the broker a file that can survive a shift change

Use one row per vehicle. Include chassis identifier, manufacturer, variant, propulsion evidence, seating, condition, origin country, tariff code, order number and requested quantity. Capture the selling entity and acquiring distributor separately. Add the declaration reference, acceptance date, allocation response and ultimate destination as events occur. Give the receiving yard a read-only view showing which units customs has released and who can lift a hold. Keep price negotiations in a separate file.

At the end of a shift, reconcile the count on the carrier manifest with the broker’s accepted quantity and the vehicles physically present. A transposed character can link a valid clearance to the wrong chassis. Trace a mismatch back to the original scan, document the correction and notify the people who relied on the earlier list. Where a declaration needs amendment, let the customs representative obtain the appropriate decision. Changing an internal workbook cannot amend an official record.

Review June commitments while they can still be changed

The published schedule ends on 18 June 2027. A dealership preparing a summer campaign should distinguish orders covered by the provisional timetable from deliveries planned beyond it. Assign someone to monitor the investigation and later official acts before pricing those deliveries. The provisional measure alone cannot establish the rules for the remainder of June. Avoid building an unverified extension or an automatic reopening date into a binding customer promise.

For the next dispatch meeting, ask three colleagues for three pieces of evidence. Engineering should show which motor propels each variant. Customs should identify the origin determination, order number and declaration timetable. Sales should identify every customer commitment dependent on a successful drawing. When all three accounts agree, record the decision and its expiry time. When they differ, the discrepancy tells the team precisely which vehicles to hold, which customers to call and which question the importer must resolve.

Frequently asked questions

Can a dealer pay a surcharge to clear covered cars after the quota is exhausted?

Article 1 bars entry once the applicable quota is exhausted. The importer should agree a lawful storage or alternative plan with customs and the carrier.

Which order number applies to Turkish-origin hybrids?

Annex II assigns 090256. Confirm the vehicle falls within the safeguard and the non-preferential origin assessment supports Türkiye before submitting a request.
