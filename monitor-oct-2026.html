October 2026 26 key developments Türkiye

Turkish Trade Monitor – October 2026

October 2026 brought a substantial package of regulatory developments affecting international businesses operating in, investing in, exporting to and sourcing from Türkiye. The most consequential changes concern company capital maintenance, employee communications monitoring, children's access to social platforms, anti-money-laundering thresholds, the launch framework for Türkiye's emissions trading market and machinery safety standards aligned with EU Regulation 2023/1230.

The month also brought new obligations for businesses commercialising geographical data, generators operating unlicensed renewable energy facilities, companies participating in public tenders and manufacturers and importers facing trade-defence measures. Several reforms include compliance dates in November and December 2026, followed by further transitions in 2027 and 2028.

The overview below selects developments of practical relevance to cross-border businesses rather than attempting to reproduce every entry in the Official Gazette.

Corporate Governance, Tax & Real Estate

Existing Companies Have Until 31 December 2027 to Reach Minimum Capital Requirements

On 17 October 2026, the Ministry of Trade extended by one year the deadline for certain existing Turkish companies to increase capital to the statutory minimum levels set under the Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102).

The applicable amounts are TRY 250,000 for joint-stock companies (anonim şirket) and TRY 50,000 for limited liability companies (limited şirket). Non-public joint-stock companies using the registered-capital system are subject to a TRY 500,000 initial-capital threshold, with additional consequences under the transitional provision. Companies below the relevant thresholds must complete their adjustment by 31 December 2027, rather than 31 December 2026. The Turkish Commercial Code provides for dissolution consequences if a company fails to comply within the prescribed period. Special meeting and voting rules apply to the required capital increases.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign-owned Turkish subsidiaries should review registered capital, shareholders' resolutions, funding arrangements and Trade Registry filings now. The extension provides additional time, but it does not alter the minimum amounts or make a capital increase automatic.
Corporate Governance, Tax & Real Estate

Foreign-Exchange Loss Relief in Capital Impairment Tests Extended into 2027

A 10 October 2026 amendment to the communiqué governing Article 376 of the Turkish Commercial Code extends the temporary accounting treatment for assessing capital loss and over-indebtedness until 1 January 2028.

For the purposes defined by that communiqué, companies may disregard the entire amount of foreign-exchange losses arising from foreign-currency liabilities not yet performed, together with half of certain accrued lease, depreciation and personnel expenses from 2020 and 2021. The specified amounts must be calculated without double-counting and disclosed in the notes rather than recorded as adjustments in the financial statements prepared under the relevant provision.

TTL INSIGHT — Companies with sizeable foreign-currency liabilities should distinguish the statutory Article 376 assessment from ordinary financial reporting. Directors should document calculations and evaluate whether separate recapitalisation or creditor measures remain necessary. This is a limited calculation relief, not a cancellation of liabilities.
Corporate Governance, Tax & Real Estate

Revised Electronic Tax-Notification Regime Adds New Obligated Taxpayers

Tax Procedure Law General Communiqué No. 596, published on 11 October 2026, replaces Communiqué No. 456 and updates the electronic tax-notification system operating through the Digital Tax Office.

The mandatory categories include corporate income taxpayers; taxpayers with commercial, agricultural or professional income assessed under the actual method; and now collective and ordinary limited partnership companies. Existing persons within the mandatory categories who had not yet joined must register by 30 November 2026. Newly established taxpayers in these groups generally have 15 days after commencing tax liability. A legally effective electronic notification is deemed served at the end of the fifth day following delivery into the system, regardless of whether an optional email or SMS alert reaches the recipient. Certain exemptions and special procedures apply, including in liquidation and bankruptcy.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign investors with Turkish entities should verify Digital Tax Office access rights, authorised representatives, login continuity and internal escalation procedures. Missing a notification can trigger a procedural deadline even when no alert email is received.
Corporate Governance, Tax & Real Estate

Mandatory Secure Payment for Real-Estate Transfers Postponed to December

On 1 October 2026, an amendment to the Regulation on Real Estate Trade moved the end of the transitional exemption from mandatory use of the secure-payment system for real-estate sales to 1 December 2026.

An integrator is to be authorised by the Ministry of Trade to operate the system. The scope of transactions excluded from the system, the pilot process, fees and fee allocation are to be determined by the relevant implementing protocol involving the Ministry of Trade, the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change and the integrator.

TTL INSIGHT — Overseas buyers, investors and Turkish real-estate intermediaries should confirm the applicable payment process well before signing and completion. Contractual mechanics should identify when purchase money is deposited, verified and released. The implementing protocol should be checked before assuming that a particular transaction must use the system.
Corporate Governance, Tax & Real Estate

Retail Instalment Rules Move from Fixed Regulatory Limits to Ministry Decisions

An amendment published on 15 October 2026 restructures Article 12/B of the Regulation on Principles and Rules Applicable to Retail Trade. It removes the former instalment-period limits from the regulation and authorises the Ministry of Trade to determine instalment durations and additional restrictions by decisions published on its website.

The amendment concerns instalment sales of goods and services other than residential property, including arrangements where payment is deferred or instalments are created after the sale for a charge. The former regulation had contained sector-specific periods for categories such as electronic goods, white goods, furniture, travel and vehicles. The amendment itself does not set replacement instalment periods.

TTL INSIGHT — Retailers, marketplaces, consumer-electronics distributors and financing partners should not interpret the deletion of old figures as unrestricted instalment sales. They should track current Ministry decisions and update checkout terms, advertising and financing agreements accordingly.
Data Protection, Digital Platforms & Location Data

Personal Data Protection Board Tightens Standards for Employer Monitoring of Business Communications

A principle decision of the Personal Data Protection Board (Decision No. 2026/2035, dated 16 September 2026), published on 8 October 2026, explains how Turkish employers may monitor corporate email accounts and other work-related communication channels under the Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 6698).

The principles extend, depending on the circumstances, to internal messaging, work chat, customer relationship management tools and collaboration-platform records. Monitoring traffic and log data is itself personal-data processing. Employers must provide sufficiently specific advance information about the purposes, legal basis, scope, access to message content, retention and data-subject rights; a general statement that company email “may be monitored” is insufficient. The Board expects a staged, proportionate approach, with access to content only where less intrusive checks are inadequate and concrete suspicion warrants it. Secret or indiscriminate continuous monitoring is not permitted. Access and retention must be limited, and former employees' accounts require controlled closure and business-continuity safeguards.

TTL INSIGHT — International employers should revisit Turkish employee privacy notices, acceptable-use policies, email investigation protocols, HR disciplinary procedures and third-party IT access. Corporate ownership of an account or device does not, by itself, authorise unrestricted reading of messages.
Data Protection, Digital Platforms & Location Data

Social Networks Must Introduce Age Verification and Special Child-Safety Controls

The Regulation on Age Verification and Differentiated Services for Children, published on 10 October 2026, applies to social-network providers accessible from Türkiye, including overseas platforms. Its principal provisions take effect on 1 November 2026.

Providers may not offer social-network services to children under 15 and must provide differentiated services for users aged 15 to 17. Age verification uses a token generated through Türkiye's e-Government identity service indicating whether the relevant age thresholds are met. Among the requirements for eligible child accounts are privacy-protective defaults, limitations on profiling and personalised commercial advertising, restricted messaging and recommendations, parental controls and accessible objection procedures. Existing accounts must undergo at least one verification by 31 March 2027. Separate rules address users temporarily present in Türkiye and certain account-verification exceptions.

TTL INSIGHT — International social-media providers, platform operators and advertisers should assess whether their services fall within the regulation and map the resulting product, identity-verification, privacy, retention, parental-control and appeal requirements. A purely contractual age declaration is not the same as the prescribed verification method.
Data Protection, Digital Platforms & Location Data

Commercial Use of Regulated Geographical Data Now Requires a Ministry Permit

The new Geographical Data Permits Regulation, published on 14 October 2026, requires natural persons and private companies — including foreign persons and foreign legal entities — to obtain a permit before commercially collecting, producing, sharing or selling geographical data covered by the National Geographical Data Responsibility Matrix.

Applications are made electronically before the activity begins. The regime includes professional-qualification requirements, administrative documentation, permit fees determined by activity and geographical scope, permits valid for one to five years, annual reporting and notifications of corporate changes. Listed exemptions cover certain routine address disclosures and licensed utility operators acting within their regulated activities. Existing permits and licences remain valid for their stated terms. Unauthorised activity can result in substantial administrative fines; the regulation applies a distinct calculation for foreign operators.

TTL INSIGHT — GIS providers, mapping platforms, location-data vendors, infrastructure surveyors and international data businesses should classify the data and activities before engaging Turkish customers. The permit requirement is not a general restriction on all address information; it applies to the defined geographical-data regime and its specific exceptions.
Financial Services, AML & Regulated Markets

MASAK Raises Transaction Identification Thresholds and Adjusts Verification Methods

A 7 October 2026 amendment to the Regulation on Measures Regarding Prevention of Laundering Proceeds of Crime and Financing of Terrorism increased a number of monetary thresholds relevant to obliged entities.

The general customer-identification threshold for a single or connected transaction increased from TRY 185,000 to TRY 370,000. The corresponding thresholds for crypto-asset service providers and relevant electronic or crypto-asset transfers increased from TRY 15,000 to TRY 30,000. The threshold associated with originator information and verification in transfer messages also rose to TRY 30,000. Under defined safeguards, financial institutions may avoid taking a fresh signature specimen in certain subsequent face-to-face transactions with existing customers where secure online, mobile or verified one-time-code authentication is used.

TTL INSIGHT — Banks, payment firms, crypto-asset service providers and other obliged entities should update thresholds across onboarding, transaction monitoring, operational procedures and internal control testing. Monetary thresholds do not remove continuing identification, enhanced due diligence or suspicious-transaction obligations where otherwise applicable.
Financial Services, AML & Regulated Markets

Special Law Introduces Liquidation and Recovery Mechanisms for Certain Investment Funds

Law No. 7596, published on 27 October 2026, establishes a bespoke liquidation regime for investment funds ordered into liquidation by the Capital Markets Board's decisions of 17 September 2026, where liquidation remained incomplete on publication. It is not a new general regime for every Turkish investment fund.

The law regulates oversight of fund liquidation, valuation, recovery of improperly transferred assets and payments to eligible unit-holders. The Capital Markets Board may permit specified interim payments of up to TRY 1 million per eligible unit-holder, subject to conditions and available funds. The portfolio management company bears first-line liability for certain fund losses, followed by potentially personal liability for identified parties if recovery from the company is unavailable. The law is stated to operate with effect from 17 September 2026.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign investors and fund stakeholders should determine whether a particular fund is covered before relying on these protections. Unit-holder status, blocked orders, repayment priority and recovery rights depend on the law's narrow scope and the Board's implementation decisions.
Financial Services, AML & Regulated Markets

Turkish Mercantile Exchange Can Trade a Broader Range of Products and Contracts

Amendments published on 8 October 2026 expand the Turkish Mercantile Exchange's framework beyond electronic warehouse receipts (ELÜS) and ELÜS-based futures. The revised rules contemplate physical products, permitted futures contracts and other instruments authorised by the Ministry of Trade, with settlement and delivery procedures set through exchange rules.

The start date on which specified capital-market intermediaries may be authorised to intermediate on the exchange was postponed from 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2028. The Ministry may bring that date forward for certain futures contracts. Any authorised intermediary must also meet the exchange's membership requirements before conducting activity.

TTL INSIGHT — Agricultural commodity traders, licensed warehouses, brokers and international buyers should monitor exchange by-laws and the product-specific settlement framework. The extended date concerns designated intermediaries; it should not be understood as a suspension of every exchange activity.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

Türkiye Establishes the Operating Framework for Its Emissions Trading Market

The Regulation on the Operation of the Turkish Emissions Trading System Market, published on 21 October 2026, establishes the operating arrangements for Türkiye's emissions trading system (ETS) market and the relevant transaction registry under the Climate Law (Law No. 7552) and electricity-market legislation.

EPİAŞ acts as market operator. Emissions allowances, each representing one tonne of carbon-dioxide-equivalent emissions over the relevant period, may be allocated through primary-market auctions or the applicable free-allocation process and traded through spot and potentially futures markets. Covered installations must establish the appropriate registry accounts, enter into the necessary participation arrangements and provide specified company and authorisation records. The regulation also allows a virtual application before the live registry and allowance markets commence, without creating actual delivery, payment or invoicing obligations during that stage.

TTL INSIGHT — Energy-intensive manufacturers, power producers and industrial investors should identify whether their installations are within the ETS scope, prepare validated emissions data and allocate responsibility for registry onboarding, allowance strategy and contractual carbon-cost allocation. The regulation establishes market mechanics; it does not mean that every business immediately needs to purchase allowances.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

Unlicensed Renewable Generators Face New Emergency Remote-Control Requirements

Amendments published on 19 October 2026 enable transmission and distribution system operators to issue and implement emergency instructions affecting certain unlicensed electricity generation installations, including relevant solar plants. The revised rules address remote operation of facilities with an operating electrical installed capacity of 900 kW or more, beginning with larger installations, and require certain aggregators to establish remote-control capabilities.

Affected generators must apply to the network operator to revise their distribution connection agreement by 18 December 2026. A related Energy Market Regulatory Board decision introduces contractual penalties linked to SCADA connection, remote switching and equipment failures. Two specified penalties will not apply until 30 April 2027. The rules also govern notification after an emergency intervention and potential loss or recovery of specified payments where curtailment instructions are not followed.

TTL INSIGHT — Solar developers, unlicensed generators, aggregators and industrial self-generators should confirm the technical scope, connection status, SCADA readiness and responsibility for equipment and communications reliability. Connection-agreement amendments should be reviewed alongside EPC, O&M and aggregation contracts.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

Electricity Distribution Security Requirements and Battery Connection Rules Revised

Further 19 October 2026 amendments alter the security-deposit framework for distribution-system users. Where a user has paid its system-use invoices in full for the preceding 24 months without defaulting on security obligations, the applicable maximum security multiplier is reduced to one times the relevant amount. Qualifying excess security must be returned within one month of the amendment, no later than 19 November 2026. The rules also cover irrevocable bank guarantees, electronic guarantee letters and deadlines for increasing or returning security.

The same package clarifies the connection conditions for certain storage facilities, including facilities whose installed storage capacity exceeds the contracted power and therefore require technical controls preventing unauthorised export or excessive grid draw.

TTL INSIGHT — Industrial customers and energy investors should audit payment histories, existing guarantees and cash-collateral balances. Battery projects should confirm grid operator approval, metering arrangements and automatic power-limitation equipment before procurement and construction.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

Technical Review Rules for Wind Power Applications Become More Specific

An amendment published on 3 October 2026 updates the technical assessment of wind-power generation applications. It specifies circumstances in which relevant applications, including those for unlicensed generation, must be refused because of incomplete or incorrect documents, conflict with other generation or designated renewable-energy areas, incompatibility with turbine type certificates, interference with an existing project or location outside approved land boundaries.

The amended provisions identify supporting land documents for registered plots, cadastral roads and waterways, and mining licence areas. Relevant network operators must submit the documentation through the designated electronic system.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign wind developers and equipment suppliers should test site coordinates, title or land-use evidence, turbine models and potential overlap with existing wind, solar and other renewable-energy rights before committing to land, turbines or permit-stage expenditures.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

Electricity-Market Transparency Rules Add Tight Disclosure Timelines

On 20 October 2026, the Energy Market Regulatory Authority determined categories of inside information and market data to be published under the rules on transparency and market abuse in energy and environmental markets. In the electricity market, the bulletin identifies thresholds of 30 MW per generation facility and 50 MW per portfolio for relevant disclosures.

Changes in available capacity, including outages and maintenance, must generally be disclosed through the designated internal information platform within one hour after the event occurs. Other information, such as final annual outage plans, must be published within the applicable timeframe. A separate decision revising data on the Transparency Platform takes effect on 1 January 2027.

TTL INSIGHT — Power producers, traders and aggregators should review event-detection, outage reporting and approval chains. Disclosure controls should be documented and coordinated with market conduct, confidentiality obligations and trading restrictions.
Energy, Climate & Industrial Infrastructure

New 2027 Electricity Consumption Limits Affect Ultimate-Supply Tariff Exposure

An Energy Market Regulatory Board decision published on 31 October 2026 sets electricity consumption thresholds for the ultimate-supply tariff effective 1 January 2027. The figures reported in the Gazette materials are 3,000 kWh per year for residential consumers, 150 million kWh per year for certain listed categories including specified agricultural consumers, and 15,000 kWh per year for other customer groups, subject to the decision's detailed classifications and exceptions.

The decision retains the specified tariff coefficients of 1.05 for the residential group and 1.0938 for other groups.

TTL INSIGHT — Businesses with sites in Türkiye should model tariff exposure by metering point and applicable subscriber group. The threshold is a tariff-classification measure, not a cap on electricity that can legally be consumed.
Trade, Technical Compliance & Public Procurement

New Machinery Safety Regulation Aligns Turkish Requirements with EU Regulation 2023/1230

The Machinery Safety Regulation (2023/1230/EU), published on 16 October 2026, establishes an updated framework for machinery and related products in Türkiye, based on EU Regulation 2023/1230. It covers machinery, interchangeable equipment, safety components, lifting accessories, chains, ropes, belts, removable mechanical transmission devices and partly completed machinery.

The regulation differentiates the duties of manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers and distributors, and establishes conformity-assessment routes for specified high-risk categories. Some institutional and transitional provisions took effect upon publication; most substantive provisions take effect on 20 January 2027, when the earlier machinery framework is repealed. Products lawfully placed on the market under the prior rules before that date benefit from the relevant transitional treatment; existing EC type-examination certificates continue for their validity periods.

TTL INSIGHT — Overseas manufacturers exporting machinery into Türkiye should revisit technical files, risk assessments, declarations, local economic-operator responsibilities and conformity-assessment strategies. The staggered commencement matters for goods already placed on the market and for contracts extending into 2027.
Trade, Technical Compliance & Public Procurement

Definitive Anti-Dumping Duties Imposed on Chinese-Origin Sodium Gluconate

Communiqué No. 2026/31 on the Prevention of Unfair Competition in Imports, published on 14 October 2026, introduced definitive anti-dumping measures on specified sodium gluconate originating in China, classified under GTİP 2918.16.00.00.13.

The reported duties, calculated as a percentage of CIF value, are 24.43% for Shandong Fuyang Bio-Tech, 22.56% for Weifang Jianbao Biotechnology and 32.96% for other producers/exporters. The measure takes effect on publication and is stated to expire after five years unless a timely expiry-review investigation preserves it under the relevant rules.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign chemical manufacturers, Turkish importers and industrial purchasers should verify the customs tariff classification, actual origin, named producer and customs value before setting prices or allocating import-duty risk. The duty applies to the defined product and measure, not automatically to every gluconate-based chemical or food ingredient.
Trade, Technical Compliance & Public Procurement

Public Procurement Debt Threshold Rises to TRY 50,000

An amendment to the Public Procurement General Communiqué, published on 16 October 2026, increases the relevant finalised social-security premium and tax-debt thresholds used when assessing bidders' eligibility in procurements under the Public Procurement Law (Law No. 4734).

The figures increase from TRY 5,000 to TRY 50,000, with the computation of outstanding tax or social-security obligations continuing to be governed by the underlying rules. The amendment comes into force on 15 November 2026. Under the transitional provision, tenders announced or formally notified before that date continue under the rules in force at the announcement or notification date.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign suppliers bidding directly or through Turkish entities should check documentary evidence of tax and social-security compliance, tender publication dates and eligibility conditions. A revised threshold is not a blanket waiver of all public-debt restrictions or a substitute for up-to-date tender documents.
Trade, Technical Compliance & Public Procurement

Patent-Filing Fee Relief Introduced for Qualifying SMEs and AI Applications

A 31 October 2026 amendment to the Turkish Patent and Trademark Office's 2026 Fee Tariff introduces preferential fees for specified applicants and technologies.

Legal entities classified as SMEs and holding the required SME Status Certificate can pay one-quarter of the ordinary electronic application, search-report and examination-report fees for their first patent application, subject to the detailed eligibility conditions. For patent applications in IPC class G06N, relating to artificial intelligence, the expedited search-report fee was reduced from TRY 8,666.67 to TRY 4,333.34. The tariff also revises the description of certain competition-related exemptions.

TTL INSIGHT — Technology start-ups, R&D ventures and overseas founders establishing Turkish innovation companies should check entity-level SME status and the novelty of the filing. Fee relief does not replace substantive patentability, prior-art searching or an international filing strategy.
Trade, Technical Compliance & Public Procurement

Organisation of Turkic States Digital Economy Agreement Ratified

A Presidential Decision published on 31 October 2026 approved the Digital Economy Partnership Agreement concluded within the Organisation of Turkic States framework. The agreement, signed in Bishkek on 6 November 2024, involves Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Türkiye and Uzbekistan.

The framework addresses cooperation on electronic commerce, paperless trading and single-window systems, electronic payments, digital identity, electronic signatures and digital certification services. Ratification in Türkiye should not be confused with confirmation that every operational provision is already in force between all participating states; the agreement's entry-into-force conditions and domestic implementation must be checked separately.

TTL INSIGHT — Businesses planning cross-border e-commerce, digital services or logistics across the region should monitor the agreement's implementation rather than assuming immediate mutual recognition of every digital document or signature.
Environmental, Healthcare & Industrial Operations

Environmental Management Service Agreements Face Revised Minimum Time and Fee Rules

An amendment to the Regulation on Environmental Management Services, published on 6 October 2026, sets new minimum service periods and remuneration for environmental consultancy services used by facilities under Türkiye's environmental permit and licence regime.

The bulletin specifies at least two working days per month for Annex 1 facilities and one working day for Annex 2 facilities; specified minimum fees are linked to fractions of the net minimum wage, excluding VAT, with additions connected to permit and licence categories. Relevant existing agreements must generally be brought into line by 1 January 2027, unless the existing remuneration already meets the required minimum. The amendment also strengthens notification and remediation procedures for environmental non-compliance and sets personnel, professional-registration and ownership conditions for consultancy firms.

TTL INSIGHT — Industrial operators and international manufacturers should verify the category of each Turkish facility, the scope and frequency of environmental support and the consultant's qualifications. Outsourcing the service does not remove the operator's underlying environmental permit, EIA or licence obligations.
Environmental, Healthcare & Industrial Operations

Electronic Informed Consent Framework Introduced for Healthcare Facilities

The Regulation on Obtaining Informed Consent Electronically, published on 27 October 2026, allows relevant healthcare facilities to obtain patient consent through a structured electronic process using e-Nabız or an authorised facility system and single-use SMS verification.

Where electronic consent is used, the patient's identity and the responsible healthcare professional's identity must be verified. The patient is given the intervention-specific information and a single-use code to confirm consent for that intervention. The process must retain evidence of the time and nature of approval, verification events and other security records under applicable retention rules. There are limitations for minors and persons under legal guardianship where a representative is absent or cannot be authenticated.

TTL INSIGHT — Private hospitals, healthcare groups, health-technology vendors and their data processors should review the consent workflow, patient notices, audit trails, access control, retention and system integrations. Electronic consent is not a substitute for obtaining informed, intervention-specific agreement in accordance with patient-rights rules.
Environmental, Healthcare & Industrial Operations

Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Facilities May Produce Additional Regulated Product Categories

A 21 October 2026 amendment to the Regulation on Manufacturing Facilities for Medicinal Products for Human Use expands the additional product categories that may be produced at pharmaceutical manufacturing sites under specified separation requirements.

Subject to an independent area, separate equipment and separate ventilation, eligible categories now include certain cannabis-derived products, foods for special medical purposes, aromatherapeutic products and herbal teas. The amendment also addresses certain traditional herbal medicinal products in the permitted manufacturing arrangements and revises the criteria for responsible managers at facilities producing human-blood- or plasma-derived medicines.

TTL INSIGHT — International pharmaceutical, biotechnology and consumer-health businesses considering contract manufacturing in Türkiye should analyse the regulatory classification of each product, GMP scope, site permissions and physical segregation. Permission to manufacture within a facility does not automatically authorise placing every such product on the Turkish market.
Environmental, Healthcare & Industrial Operations

Shipyards and Slipways Face Updated Operating Permit and Safety Requirements

Amendments published on 27 October 2026 revise rules for shipyards and slipway facilities. An operating permit may be issued for up to five years, with at least one inspection during its validity. Following a facility transfer or lease, the acquirer or lessee may, if conditions are met, receive a one-off partial operating permit lasting two years.

The occupational health and safety certification reference is updated from OHSAS 18001 to TS EN ISO 45001. Operators must record specified construction, maintenance, repair and dry-docking information in the relevant ministry information system. A transitional exception applies to certain applications filed before 1 January 2027 in respect of site and mooring plans.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign maritime investors, shipyard operators, repair contractors and lessors should address permit continuity in acquisitions and lease agreements, review certification obligations and confirm that technical records and operating conditions are maintained following a change of operator.
Practical next steps

What International Businesses Should Do Next

October's developments call for entity-, activity- and product-specific compliance reviews. International groups should check the capital position of Turkish subsidiaries; revise HR monitoring and data-processing procedures; update transaction monitoring and tax-notification access; assess the applicability of child-safety and geographical-data permit rules; and prepare for the staggered commencement of machinery and energy-market obligations.

Industrial businesses should review environmental service contracts, site approvals, grid connections and emissions data. Importers should revisit tariff classifications, product documentation and duties, while businesses pursuing public contracts should verify bidder eligibility and relevant tender dates. Assign a responsible team member to each deadline and retain evidence of the review.
