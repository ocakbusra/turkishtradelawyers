Repatriating Profits from Türkiye

“Can a foreign investor transfer dividends and profits out of Türkiye?”

Quick Answer

Türkiye’s Foreign Direct Investment Law permits foreign investors to transfer abroad, through banks or other authorised financial institutions, specified net profits, dividends, sale and liquidation proceeds, compensation, licence and management fees, and qualifying loan principal and interest. This transfer right does not remove company-approval, tax/withholding, treaty, transfer-pricing or bank-documentation requirements. See Law No. 4875 on Foreign Direct Investment and check the current tax treatment for the payment.

What “Profit Repatriation” Covers

Foreign investors commonly use “profit repatriation” to mean moving value from a Turkish investment to an overseas shareholder or group company. The legal route depends on the payment: dividend, branch profit, sale proceeds, liquidation distribution, royalty, service or management fee, compensation, or repayment of an eligible loan. Each route has its own corporate, tax, contract and evidence requirements.

Article 3 of Law No. 4875 on Foreign Direct Investment recognises the transfer abroad through banks or other authorised financial institutions of certain net profits, dividends, sale and liquidation proceeds, compensation, licence and management fees, and principal and interest on foreign loans. This statutory transfer framework should not be read as a tax exemption or automatic entitlement to transfer an unapproved or undocumented payment.

Dividend Transfers from a Turkish Company

A dividend is a corporate distribution, not simply a bank transfer labelled “profit.” The company should establish distributable profits under its financial statements and applicable accounting rules, satisfy statutory and articles-based reserve requirements, obtain the required general-assembly approval and document the amount and recipient. The resolution, accounts and payment instruction should be consistent.

Withholding tax may apply, and the rate can depend on the recipient, payment character, applicable domestic rules, tax treaty eligibility and documentation. A treaty claim may require a valid certificate of tax residence and evidence of beneficial ownership or other conditions. Confirm the rate at the time of payment; avoid relying on historic rates or a group’s prior-year treatment.

Royalties, Service Fees and Intra-Group Payments

Royalties, management fees and service charges should correspond to actual rights or services, a written agreement and evidence of performance. Related-party payments may raise transfer-pricing, deductibility, withholding and VAT questions. A bank may request an invoice, agreement, board approval, tax documentation, service evidence or explanation of the payment purpose.

Use a payment file that connects the contract, invoice, deliverable, tax treatment, accounting entry and bank instruction. A transfer labelled as a “management fee” does not establish that the service was performed or that the amount is deductible or treaty-protected.

Sale and Liquidation Proceeds

Proceeds from selling Turkish shares or assets and distributions after liquidation are recognised transfer categories under the foreign-investment framework, but the transaction itself may trigger tax, corporate, regulatory, creditor or registry requirements. For a share sale, review the seller’s tax position, transaction documents, escrow or holdback, foreign-exchange conversion and bank evidence. For liquidation, the company must complete the creditor-protection process and calculate what remains available for distribution.

Bank Documentation and Transfer Controls

Transfers are processed through the banking or other authorised financial channel. Requirements vary by bank, payment category, amount, beneficiary and current controls. Be prepared to provide corporate approvals, audited or management accounts, tax receipts or withholding evidence, contracts, invoices, transfer-pricing support, shareholder records, source-of-funds information and recipient account details.

Keep the amount, currency, payment purpose and recipient consistent across the resolution, tax filing, accounting records and remittance instruction. If a transfer is rejected or held for review, ask the bank what evidence is missing and obtain tax or legal advice before changing the payment description.

Foreign Investor Transfer Checklist

  1. Classify the payment as dividend, service fee, royalty, sale proceeds, liquidation distribution or loan repayment.
  2. Confirm the company or contract has authority to make the payment and obtain the correct corporate approval.
  3. Calculate current tax and withholding treatment; assess treaty eligibility and gather supporting certificates.
  4. For related-party charges, retain contracts, evidence of performance, pricing support and required tax documentation.
  5. Coordinate bank documentation, currency, beneficiary and timing before instructing the transfer.
  6. Reconcile the payment to accounting, tax and statutory filings and retain the complete remittance record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a foreign investor transfer dividends from Türkiye?

The foreign-investment framework permits specified dividend transfers through authorised financial channels, subject to corporate approval, tax and documentation requirements.

Does the transfer right mean there are no taxes or bank checks?

No. Tax, withholding, treaty eligibility, source-of-funds review, corporate approvals and bank documentation remain relevant.

Can a Turkish company distribute all accounting profit?

Not automatically. Distributable profit, reserves, financial statements, company form and required shareholder approvals must be checked.

Are royalties and management fees treated like dividends?

No. They are different payment types and may have separate contract, withholding, transfer-pricing, VAT and evidence requirements.

Can a foreign shareholder receive liquidation proceeds?

A lawful residual distribution may be transferred after the liquidation and creditor-protection steps, subject to tax, corporate and bank requirements.

Primary source: Foreign Direct Investment Law No. 4875.
