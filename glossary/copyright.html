Copyright Protection in Türkiye

"How is copyright protected in Turkey?"

Quick Answer

Copyright in a qualifying work arises without a registration application. Law No. 5846 gives authors moral and economic rights, but some film, music and computer-game productions have separate mandatory registration procedures. For business use, confirm the creator, chain of title, licensed rights and permitted territory before publishing or distributing the work.

Which Works Are Protected?

Law No. 5846 on Intellectual and Artistic Works (FSEK) protects qualifying literary and scientific, musical, fine-art and cinematographic works, as well as qualifying adaptations and collections. A work must carry its author's individual characteristics. The law protects the expression, not an idea in the abstract. Article 2 expressly covers computer programs and preparatory designs that lead to a program, but not the ideas and principles behind a program or interface.

For a company, the first practical question is not only whether the material is protected, but who created it and which rights the company may exercise. The author is generally the natural person who created the work. Employment and commissioning arrangements can affect the exercise of economic rights; a buyer should review the relevant contracts instead of assuming that payment alone transferred every right.

Business Rights-Clearance Checklist

  • Identify every creator and any earlier employer, agency, publisher or licensor in the chain of title.
  • Check whether reproduction, adaptation, distribution, public performance and digital communication rights are expressly covered.
  • State territory, duration, permitted channels, exclusivity, sublicensing and whether source files may be modified.
  • Keep dated source files, contracts, approvals and publication records as evidence.

Is Registration Required?

Copyright protection starts when a qualifying work is created; voluntary registration is not a condition for protection. The Ministry of Culture and Tourism explains that voluntary registration can help evidence authorship. Separate mandatory registration rules apply to certain film and music productions and computer games. These administrative requirements should not be confused with the creation of copyright itself.

Who Owns and May Use the Rights?

Authors have moral rights, including decisions about disclosure, attribution and certain changes to the work. Economic rights include adaptation, reproduction, distribution, performance and communication to the public. A company may obtain or exercise economic rights under the applicable law and contracts, while moral rights require separate attention. Multiple contributors, employee-created work and incorporated third-party material make the chain of title more complex.

Article 52 requires contracts and dispositions concerning economic rights to be in writing and to specify the rights individually. A licence should therefore say exactly what may be copied, adapted, sold, streamed or shared, and for how long and where. Buying a physical artwork or a source-code copy does not by itself transfer all intellectual rights.

How Long Does Protection Last?

The general term for an author's economic rights is the author's lifetime plus 70 years, calculated under the rules of Law No. 5846. Different starting points and special rules can apply to anonymous works, works disclosed after death and related rights. Do not treat an unknown publication date as proof that a work is in the public domain.

Exceptions and Permissions

Turkish law contains specific limitations, including conditions for quotation, education and personal use. It does not provide a general US-style “fair use” defence that makes any short excerpt or non-commercial use automatically lawful. Check the applicable statutory exception, purpose, extent of use and attribution; otherwise obtain a licence. Software and audiovisual uses can involve additional rights and exceptions.

What If a Work Is Used Without Permission?

Available civil routes can include stopping or preventing infringement, claims relating to unauthorised use of economic rights and damages where the statutory conditions are met. Article 68 allows a right holder, in covered circumstances, to claim an amount up to three times the hypothetical contractual fee or applicable value; it is not an automatic award in every dispute. Preserve the work, licence history, infringing copies, dates and evidence of commercial use before choosing a remedy.

Official Sources

See the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's English translation of Law No. 5846, its guidance on protection and voluntary registration, and its mandatory-registration guidance. Check the Turkish text and current procedure for a transaction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does copyright arise automatically in Türkiye, and is registration required?

Protection of a qualifying work arises on creation without voluntary registration. Separate mandatory registration procedures apply to certain film and music productions and computer games; registration does not itself create copyright.

How long does copyright last in Türkiye?

The general term for an individual author’s economic rights is the author’s lifetime plus 70 years. Special starting points or terms can apply to particular works and related rights.

What does the three-times amount in Article 68 mean?

For covered unauthorised uses, Law No. 5846 Article 68 permits a right holder to claim up to three times a hypothetical contractual fee or applicable value. The amount and remedy depend on the facts and statutory conditions.

Are computer programs protected under Turkish copyright law?

Yes. Article 2 covers computer programs and qualifying preparatory designs. The ideas and principles underlying a program or its interface are not protected as works merely because they are used in software.

Who owns copyright in work created for a company?

The natural-person creator is generally the author. The company’s ability to exercise economic rights depends on the employment or commission arrangement, applicable law and any written licence or assignment.

What must a Turkish copyright assignment or licence specify?

Under Article 52, contracts and dispositions concerning economic rights must be in writing and specify each right individually. The agreement should also define the scope of permitted use, territory, duration and sublicensing.
