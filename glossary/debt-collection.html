Debt Collection

"How does debt collection work in Turkey?"

Quick Answer

For an unpaid B2B invoice in Turkey, first confirm the debtor, amount and supporting evidence. Depending on the documents and any dispute, recovery may begin with an amicable demand, continue through a Turkish enforcement office or require litigation. An objection to enforcement without a judgment can stop that route until the creditor takes further legal steps.

What Does Debt Collection Mean for a Foreign Creditor?

Debt collection is the process of seeking payment of an overdue obligation. For a foreign supplier, lender or service provider, the first issue is usually factual: which Turkish legal entity owes the money, what triggered payment, and whether the counterparty disputes delivery, quality, price or authority to sign. A demand letter cannot cure a weak evidence file.

The main statutory framework for enforcement proceedings is Turkey's Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004. The underlying contract and transaction also matter. A court judgment, a qualifying instrument and an unpaid invoice may call for different routes. There is no single procedure that guarantees payment merely because an invoice is overdue.

Documents to Assemble Before Taking Action

  • The signed contract, purchase orders and amendments, including the payment and dispute clauses.
  • Invoices, account statements, payment history and the calculation of the outstanding balance.
  • Delivery records, acceptance certificates, service reports or correspondence showing performance.
  • Reminders, acknowledgments, objections and any settlement discussions.
  • The debtor's registered name, address and available information about assets or security.

Which Recovery Route Fits the Claim?

Amicable resolution: A documented payment request can clarify the amount, invite a response and leave room for a negotiated payment plan. The creditor should preserve evidence and review notice requirements before sending a termination or default notice.

Enforcement proceedings: Turkish enforcement offices administer monetary debt proceedings. An enforcement route without a prior judgment may be available, but a debtor's timely objection can halt that proceeding and require the creditor to assess an action to overcome the objection. A judgment-based route has different prerequisites. The correct filing, jurisdiction and documents need case-specific review.

Litigation or another dispute process: Where liability or the amount is genuinely contested, the contract's court or arbitration clause and any applicable pre-action requirements may shape the next step. A foreign judgment or arbitral award raises a separate recognition or enforcement question.

Risks to Check Before Spending on Recovery

Check whether the debtor is the correct contracting entity, whether set-off or defective-performance allegations exist, whether the claim may be time-barred, and whether identifiable assets or security make recovery realistic. Consider the cost of translation, service, proceedings and potential objections alongside the debt value. Interest and costs should be calculated from the applicable contract and law, not assumed.
