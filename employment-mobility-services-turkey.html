Remote Work, EOR and Workforce Structuring

Foreign companies without a Turkish entity increasingly hire Turkish engineering, sales and support talent remotely. Remote work, Employer of Record (EOR), contractor and secondment models each create different Turkish employment, tax, work permit and social security consequences.

Remote Work Regulations

Remote work is recognized under Turkish labor law and should be documented in a written agreement covering equipment, working hours, data security, and occupational safety.

Employer of Record Models

EOR platforms can help foreign entities hire Turkish talent without a subsidiary, but joint liability and subcontracting rules must be reviewed carefully.

Mobility Exemptions

Some foreign assignments may qualify for a work permit exemption or a special mobility route. We confirm the correct authorisation before work begins.

Telecommuting Regulations in Türkiye

The remote work contract should state the job description, equipment supplied by the employer, expense treatment, working hours, communication protocols, and data protection obligations.

Occupational Health and Safety

Even in home-office arrangements, employers must inform employees about occupational risks and provide necessary training. Work-related accidents at home can still create SGK and employer liability if the arrangement is poorly documented.

Employer of Record and Subcontracting Risks

In an EOR model, the local provider is the formal employer for payroll, SGK, and tax filings, while the employee performs work for the foreign company. Turkish courts may scrutinize whether this arrangement reflects the real employment relationship.

Joint Liability Risk

If the model is treated as a collusive subcontracting structure, the foreign company may be exposed to claims for severance, overtime, unpaid wages, and social security premiums. Strong indemnities, role descriptions, and local compliance controls are essential.

Work Permit Exemptions and Mobility Routes

Some assignments and categories of foreign personnel may qualify for a work permit exemption or a special mobility route, depending on nationality, role, duration, employer and activity in Turkey.

Work Permit Boundary

A residence permission or exemption route is not automatically a Turkish work permit. Local employment, services to Turkish clients or active Turkish-market commercial activity may require a formal work permit or Turkish employment structure.

Managing Terminations & Severance Risk

Employee termination is one of the largest corporate litigation risks in Türkiye. Severance pay, notice pay, idle-time back pay, and reinstatement compensation can compound quickly if the process is not documented and executed correctly.

Step 1: Identify the Basis for Termination

Employer-initiated termination generally depends on either valid reason or just cause. Valid reason covers performance, operational redundancy, or economic restructuring and usually requires notice and severance. Just cause covers serious misconduct and has a higher proof burden.

Step 2: Calculate Severance Pay

Employees with at least one full year of service are generally entitled to severance pay unless a lawful just-cause termination applies. Severance is calculated as 30 days of gross wage for each full year of service, subject to the statutory ceiling.

Step 3: Consider Mutual Termination

A mutual termination agreement can reduce litigation risk when it provides a reasonable benefit beyond statutory minimums. The employee's consent must be voluntary and the package should be drafted with clear waiver and payment mechanics.

Step 4: Assess Reinstatement Lawsuit Exposure

Employees in protected workplaces may challenge a termination through mandatory mediation and reinstatement litigation. If the employer does not reinstate after an adverse decision, compensation and back pay exposure may follow.

Payroll, Taxation & Social Security

Payroll in Türkiye requires coordination of SGK premiums, unemployment insurance, progressive income tax, and stamp tax. Under-reporting wages or paying part of salary off-record can create retroactive premium assessments, administrative fines, and loss of incentives.

Gross-to-net planning: Turkish salaries are often negotiated on a net basis. Employers must calculate the gross wage and employer-side cost, including SGK and unemployment insurance contributions.

Progressive income tax: Wage taxation increases as the employee moves through annual tax brackets, so gross salary arrangements can reduce net pay later in the calendar year unless the package is modeled carefully.

2026 Work Permit Evaluation Criteria

Work permit approval is not based solely on the employee’s qualifications. The Ministry of Labour and Social Security applies employment, financial-capacity, wage and sector-specific criteria. These are general evaluation criteria, not a universal checklist for every application.

Employment Criterion

As a general rule, at least five Turkish citizens should be employed at the workplace for each foreign employee, subject to current exemptions and special sector, profession and status rules. For workplaces with net sales of at least TRY 50,000,000 in the previous year, the criterion is not applied for applications for up to five foreign employees.

Financial Capacity

A newly established balance-sheet-based employer generally needs TRY 500,000 paid-in capital. An operating employer may meet the general criterion through TRY 500,000 paid-in capital, TRY 8,000,000 net sales or USD 150,000 exports.

Role-Based Salary Multiples

The published general criteria use 5× gross minimum wage for senior executives and pilots, 4× for engineers and architects, 3× for other managers, 2× for expertise or craftsmanship roles, and at least minimum wage for domestic services and other jobs.

Eligibility Review

Provisions effective from 3 August 2026 introduce additional exceptions and sector-specific arrangements. We assess sponsor and candidate eligibility before filing and do not promise approval based on a simplified ratio.
