Customs and Import Legal Services in Turkey

We advise clients from the pre-import planning stage through customs clearance, post-clearance controls and customs disputes.

Our services include:

1. Release for Free Circulation and “Nationalisation” of Goods in Turkey

For imported goods to enter the Turkish market as goods in free circulation, the applicable trade policy measures and import formalities must be completed and legally due import taxes must be paid.

In Turkish commercial practice, this process is also frequently referred to as the “nationalisation” or “millileştirme” of imported goods.

We advise on the legal requirements for release for free circulation, including:

  • identifying the customs and import regime applicable to the goods;
  • reviewing whether the goods may be freely imported into Turkey;
  • determining the documents, approvals and permits required before customs entry;
  • reviewing tariff classification, origin and customs value;
  • identifying customs duty, additional customs duty and other import-related fiscal exposure;
  • analysing whether surveillance, safeguard, anti-dumping or other trade policy measures apply;
  • reviewing product safety and technical compliance requirements;
  • coordinating the legal requirements of the import with the appointed customs broker;
  • resolving legal issues that arise while the goods are awaiting customs clearance; and
  • advising on alternative customs regimes where immediate release for free circulation is not commercially suitable.

Our role is particularly valuable where the shipment involves a new product, a disputed GTIP, a high customs value, royalties or licence fees, preferential origin, technical product regulation, an additional customs duty or an import restriction.

2. Pre-Import Customs and Market Entry Review

Many customs problems should be solved before the goods leave the country of export.

Turkish Trade Lawyers can conduct a pre-import legal review to determine how a product will be treated when it reaches Turkey.

A typical review may cover:

  • proposed GTIP / HS classification;
  • applicable customs duty;
  • additional customs duty;
  • VAT and other import-related taxes or charges;
  • anti-dumping or countervailing measures;
  • safeguard measures;
  • surveillance measures;
  • tariff quotas or quantitative restrictions;
  • rules of origin;
  • preferential trade treatment;
  • A.TR, EUR.1 or other origin / circulation documentation;
  • TAREKS requirements;
  • CE marking and technical legislation;
  • product-specific import permits or approvals;
  • labelling, documentation and conformity requirements;
  • specialised customs office requirements;
  • customs valuation issues;
  • importer structure and local regulatory responsibilities; and
  • legal risks that could lead to delay, additional assessment, storage cost or rejection of the shipment.

Deliverable: A written import and customs roadmap showing what must be completed before shipment, what must be presented at customs and which legal risks should be resolved before the goods are placed on the Turkish market.

3. GTIP / HS Code and Customs Tariff Classification

Correct tariff classification is one of the most important elements of importing into Turkey.

Turkey uses a 12-digit Customs Tariff Statistics Position, known as the GTIP. The GTIP may affect not only the customs duty rate but also additional customs duties, product safety controls, import permits, surveillance measures, anti-dumping duties and other trade policy measures.

We assist clients with:

  • legal review of proposed GTIP classifications;
  • classification of technically complex or multi-component products;
  • comparison of alternative tariff positions;
  • assessment of classification-related duty and regulatory consequences;
  • review of product descriptions, technical specifications, catalogues and composition;
  • preparation and legal coordination of Binding Tariff Information applications;
  • disputes concerning tariff classification; and
  • customs audits involving alleged incorrect GTIP declarations.

For businesses importing the same product repeatedly, obtaining clarity on classification before commercial-scale imports can materially reduce customs risk.

4. Customs Valuation

The customs value declared in Turkey can directly affect the amount of import taxes payable.

Valuation becomes particularly important where the transaction includes related parties, royalties, licence fees, assists, commissions, discounts, tooling, engineering, transfer pricing adjustments or other payments connected with the imported goods.

We advise on:

  • transaction value analysis;
  • additions to and exclusions from customs value;
  • royalty and licence fee treatment;
  • related-party imports;
  • transfer pricing and customs valuation interaction;
  • assists, moulds, tools and production inputs supplied by the buyer;
  • freight, insurance and other transport-related costs;
  • post-import price adjustments;
  • retrospective customs assessments; and
  • valuation disputes with customs authorities.

Where necessary, we work alongside the client’s finance, tax, transfer pricing and customs teams to ensure that the legal position is consistent across the transaction.

5. Rules of Origin, A.TR, EUR.1 and Preferential Trade

Origin is not merely the country from which goods are shipped.

Under Turkish customs practice, origin can determine the tariff treatment of goods and the application of trade policy measures including anti-dumping, surveillance, safeguard measures and quantitative restrictions.

We advise on:

  • preferential and non-preferential origin;
  • origin documentation;
  • EUR.1 certificates;
  • A.TR movement certificates;
  • free trade agreement eligibility;
  • supplier declarations and supporting documents;
  • origin verification;
  • insufficient processing and substantial transformation issues;
  • triangular and multi-country supply chains;
  • origin implications of manufacturing in third countries;
  • retrospective origin challenges; and
  • customs disputes involving origin.

Important: An A.TR certificate demonstrates the free-circulation status of qualifying goods in EU–Turkey customs union trade; it is not, by itself, proof of the origin of the goods.

6. TAREKS, Product Safety and Technical Import Compliance

Certain products imported into Turkey are subject to technical regulation, product safety or conformity controls.

TAREKS — the Risk-Based Trade Control System — is used by the Ministry of Trade for risk-based electronic import and export controls covering relevant product groups.

We help companies identify and manage:

  • whether the product is subject to TAREKS;
  • applicable Product Safety and Inspection Communiqués;
  • CE marking requirements;
  • declarations of conformity;
  • test reports and technical files;
  • standards and conformity documentation;
  • importer obligations;
  • authorised representative issues where applicable;
  • labelling and Turkish-language documentation;
  • sector-specific ministry approvals;
  • physical inspection or document-control issues;
  • rejected or incomplete TAREKS applications; and
  • legal strategy where the goods cannot proceed through the expected conformity route.

This review is particularly important for machinery, electrical and electronic products, industrial equipment, consumer goods, medical or regulated products and other products subject to technical legislation.

7. Import Licences, Permits and Sector-Specific Approvals

Some products cannot be imported into Turkey solely by filing a customs declaration.

Depending on the product, importation may require an approval, control certificate, licence, conformity document, ministry permission or other sector-specific authorisation.

We assist with the legal analysis and coordination of import requirements involving relevant Turkish authorities, including where applicable:

  • Ministry of Trade;
  • Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry;
  • Ministry of Health;
  • Turkish Medicines and Medical Devices Agency;
  • Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change;
  • Energy Market Regulatory Authority;
  • Information and Communication Technologies Authority;
  • Turkish Standards Institution; and
  • other competent product or sector regulators.

Our review focuses on the specific product and GTIP rather than relying on generic import checklists.

8. Customs Duties, Additional Customs Duty and Import Cost Exposure

Before entering Turkey, an importer should know not only the ordinary customs duty but the full regulatory cost profile of the goods.

Depending on the product, origin and trade regime, the import may be affected by:

  • customs duty;
  • additional customs duty;
  • import VAT;
  • special consumption tax where applicable;
  • anti-dumping duty;
  • countervailing duty;
  • safeguard measures;
  • additional financial liabilities;
  • funds or other product-specific charges;
  • surveillance measures; and
  • tariff quota rules.

We conduct legal duty mapping to identify which measures may apply and why.

This is especially important because Turkish import measures are regularly amended and the cost of importing a product cannot safely be determined from the HS code used in another country alone.

9. Anti-Dumping, Safeguards, Surveillance and Trade Defence Measures

Turkey actively uses trade policy defence instruments.

A product may be commercially viable under the ordinary customs tariff but become significantly more expensive when an anti-dumping measure, safeguard measure, surveillance requirement or additional customs duty applies.

We advise importers, exporters and manufacturers on:

  • whether an existing trade defence measure applies;
  • scope of anti-dumping measures;
  • product and origin coverage;
  • anti-circumvention risk;
  • surveillance implementation;
  • safeguard measures;
  • additional customs duties;
  • investigation questionnaires and submissions;
  • legal strategy during trade defence investigations;
  • review of supplier and production structures; and
  • customs disputes arising from the application of trade defence measures.

10. Customs Broker Coordination and Customs Clearance Support

Turkish Trade Lawyers is a law firm, not a customs brokerage company.

Under Turkish customs rules, companies may carry out customs formalities through their own authorised representatives or appoint licensed customs brokers for indirect representation.

Where a client works with a customs broker, we can act as the legal layer of the customs process.

We coordinate with the broker, logistics provider, importer, exporter and technical teams on matters such as:

  • legal classification strategy;

  • customs authority requests;

This structure gives international clients a single legal point of contact while operational customs declarations remain with the appropriately authorised customs professionals.

17. Customs Appeals, Settlement and Litigation

Customs decisions can have immediate financial and operational consequences.

Under the Turkish Customs Law, administrative remedies may be available against notified customs taxes, penalties and administrative decisions. Certain customs tax and penalty disputes may also be eligible for customs settlement procedures, subject to the applicable legal conditions and procedural deadlines.

Our customs dispute practice includes:

  • administrative objections;
  • customs settlement applications;
  • legal submissions to customs authorities;
  • refund or remission applications;
  • challenges to additional assessments;
  • challenges to customs penalties;
  • tax court litigation;
  • appeals before regional administrative courts;
  • Council of State proceedings where applicable; and
  • interim strategy for goods or guarantees affected by the dispute.

Customs deadlines can be short. If your company has received a customs tax, penalty or administrative decision, legal review should begin immediately.

18. Refund and Remission of Customs Duties

Where customs duties were paid even though they were not legally due, or where an assessment should not have been made, Turkish customs legislation provides procedures for refund or remission in qualifying circumstances.

We assist with:

  • legal review of overpaid customs duties;
  • refund / remission applications;
  • correction of customs declarations where available;
  • supporting evidence;
  • classification or origin-related refund claims;
  • valuation-related refund claims; and
  • disputes following rejection of refund applications.

We draft and review international trade agreements with customs and regulatory risk in mind, including:

We recommend the reverse.

Before market entry, we can review:

  2. What is the correct GTIP?
  3. Is the product freely importable?
  4. Which taxes and trade-policy measures apply?
  5. Does origin create an additional duty or anti-dumping risk?
  6. Is A.TR, EUR.1 or another origin document required?
  7. Is TAREKS applicable?
  8. Which technical or sector-specific approvals are required?

This approach turns customs from a last-minute operational issue into a controlled market-entry process.

When Should You Contact a Customs Lawyer in Turkey?

You should consider legal review before:

  • shipping a new product to Turkey for the first time;
  • changing the supplier or country of origin;
  • using an HS / GTIP classification that has not been independently reviewed;
  • relying on A.TR or preferential origin treatment;
  • importing a product subject to TAREKS;
  • importing goods that may be subject to anti-dumping or additional customs duty;
  • entering into a new distribution or supply agreement;
  • changing transfer prices or royalty arrangements;
  • using temporary import or inward processing;
  • responding to a customs inspection or audit; or
  • accepting and paying a customs tax or penalty assessment without reviewing available remedies.

Customs legal advice is usually most valuable before the shipment is exposed to storage cost, demurrage, production delay or an administrative deadline.
