September 2026 18 key developments Türkiye

Turkish Trade Monitor – September 2026

September 2026 brought important developments for international businesses operating in, selling into, sourcing from or investing in Türkiye. The most significant changes concern Türkiye's new green taxonomy, strategic industrial financing, remote onboarding in regulated financial services, sanctions screening, online-platform tax reporting and import trade-defence measures.

Manufacturers and importers also face new rules on food flavourings, meat and dairy products, solid fuels and environmental protection. Medical-device conformity assessment, biotechnology, aviation safety and hybrid employment arrangements were affected by further changes.

For foreign investors and cross-border operators, the practical priority is to translate these developments into entity-level eligibility checks, product and supplier reviews, contract updates and a clear compliance calendar.

Investment Incentives & Industrial Development

Strategic Investment Financing Rules Open a New Route for Industrial Projects

On 3 September 2026, amendments to the Communiqué on the Strategic Priority and Technical Assessment of Investment Projects introduced a Priority Financing Certificate (Öncelikli Finansman Belgesi) and revised the rules governing the Technical and Strategic Project Certificate (TSP Belgesi).

The TSP route concerns qualifying investments under Türkiye's development and project-based state-aid frameworks, strategic product and technology priorities and critical raw-material projects. Priority Financing Certificates are principally directed at manufacturing investments with qualifying investment incentive certificates, while specified projects may have a preliminary application route without one.

The envisaged project cost must be at least TRY 1 billion for a TSP Certificate or TRY 100 million for a Priority Financing Certificate. A TSP applicant must additionally meet an equity or paid-in-capital threshold of at least TRY 50 million based on its latest financial-year balance sheet. Certain eligible incentive programmes qualify without an additional evaluation; other Priority Financing Certificate applications are subject to a scoring threshold of at least 50 points.

Applications are submitted through the Ministry of Industry and Technology's electronic platform. The revised framework also restricts simultaneous use of relevant financing programmes and the stacking of interest or profit-share support for the same financing.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign manufacturers and technology investors should examine financing-certificate eligibility alongside investment-incentive planning. Ownership structure, Turkish entity financial statements, capitalisation, project cost and strategic product classification can affect access to financing. The annexed eligible-programme and scoring lists must be checked before any application.
Investment Incentives & Industrial Development

New Industrial Zones and a Larger Adana Yumurtalık Free Zone

On 5 September 2026, Türkiye designated 109.41 hectares in Eskişehir as the Ford Otomotiv Sanayi A.Ş. Eskişehir Special Industrial Zone. Two further industrial zones were declared in Sakarya's Ferizli and Kaynarca districts. For the Sakarya zones, the relevant ministerial payment by investors was set at one per thousand of fixed investment.

Separately, the boundaries of the Adana Yumurtalık Free Zone were redetermined. The decision added approximately 1.05 million m² of land and 2.71 million m² of sea area to the previously identified zone areas, according to the maps and coordinates attached to the decision.

TTL INSIGHT — These are location-specific developments, not a general approval of every proposed investment. Manufacturing and logistics groups evaluating sites in Eskişehir, Sakarya or the Adana–Ceyhan industrial corridor should review the official boundary annexes, land rights, zoning, zone-administration requirements, permits and any investment incentives independently.
Climate & Environmental Compliance

Türkiye Publishes Its Green Taxonomy Regulation

The Türkiye Green Taxonomy Regulation, published on 24 September 2026, establishes a framework for classifying environmentally sustainable economic activities and supporting sustainable finance while addressing misleading environmental claims.

A taxonomy-aligned activity must make a substantial contribution to at least one of six environmental objectives, avoid significant harm to the other objectives and satisfy minimum social safeguards. The objectives address climate-change mitigation and adaptation, sustainable use of water and marine resources, circular economy, pollution prevention and control, and biodiversity and ecosystem protection. Production activities involving solid fossil fuels are excluded from classification as environmentally sustainable under the Regulation.

Businesses undertaking activities in Annex 1 may report taxonomy information. Financial institutions identified by the Regulation are subject to a reporting framework determined by their respective regulators and may request relevant information from underlying businesses. Reporting indicators include turnover, capital expenditure and operating expenditure, with limited exceptions. Reporting is generally submitted through an online system by the end of the sixth month after the relevant financial reporting period. The Regulation entered into force on publication, while financial institutions are not required to report until 1 January 2029.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign-owned manufacturers, project developers, lenders and investment funds should prepare reliable activity-level sustainability data and distinguish taxonomy eligibility from taxonomy alignment. Green-finance representations, corporate disclosures and financing covenants should be checked against the actual technical screening criteria, not marketing descriptions. Annex 1 and subsequent implementing criteria require separate review.
Climate & Environmental Compliance

Solid-Fuel Import, Distribution and Industrial Use Become More Tightly Controlled

On 16 September 2026, the Regulation on the Control of Solid Fuels for the Protection of Air Quality introduced an integrated framework for the quality, documentation, sale, use and inspection of imported and domestic solid fuels.

Imported fuels are subject to importer registration and a Certificate of Conformity based on testing. The Solid Fuel Module within the Air Emissions Management Portal is used to monitor domestic production, commercial movements and industrial consumption. Relevant dispatch or delivery information must generally be entered within 24 hours. Specific requirements concern petroleum coke, fuel mixing, dust management, storage, heating-fuel sales, packaging and traceability. Non-compliant fuel may be taken off the market, with environmental-law sanctions for violations.

Existing certificates remain valid until 30 June 2027. Colour-coded bags and QR-code rules commence on 1 June 2028; transitional provisions for solid-biofuel combustion systems extend to 1 June 2030. Other provisions generally entered into force on publication.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign fuel suppliers, importers, industrial users and logistics providers should audit product specifications, importer registrations, laboratory evidence, portal reporting and allocation of responsibilities in transport and supply agreements. Technical fuel limits in the Regulation's annexes must be verified product by product.
Climate & Environmental Compliance

Wetlands and Forest-Fire Protection Rules Affect Project Developers

Two regulations published on 5 September 2026 are relevant to projects with forest access, water infrastructure or sites near sensitive ecosystems.

Amendments to the Regulation on the Protection of Wetlands introduced water-abstraction permits and rules protecting environmental flows. Certain industrial zones, organised industrial zones and free zones in controlled-use areas must prepare cumulative impact assessments. The amendments also address renewable-energy facilities in artificial wetlands and a transitional permit requirement for certain privately operated water structures commissioned after 30 January 2002, with a two-year application period.

The new Regulation on Forest Firefighting Activities governs prevention, preparedness, intervention and recovery. Parties operating in forest-permitted or tourism-allocated areas may have specific preventive responsibilities, while access and operational restrictions can apply during periods of extreme fire risk.

TTL INSIGHT — Renewable-energy, hospitality, mining, real-estate, industrial and infrastructure investors should address wetland status, abstraction rights, environmental flows, cumulative impacts and wildfire precautions at site-selection and due-diligence stages. The applicable restrictions will depend on the specific land classification and project.
Financial Services, Sanctions & Digital Compliance

Capital Markets and Crypto Firms Gain New Remote-Onboarding Rules for Foreign Clients

On 3 September 2026, Communiqué III-42.1.b amended remote-identification rules for investment firms, portfolio management companies and crypto-asset service providers. It expressly addresses sole traders, authorised representatives of registered legal entities and non-Turkish individual clients.

Non-Turkish individuals may be remotely identified using an ICAO 9303-compliant NFC-enabled passport, subject to enhanced controls. If the passport chip cannot be verified, the specified process cannot be completed. The customer's address must be verified within three months; until then, certain transfers cannot proceed. Clients accepted through this passport procedure are treated as higher risk and are subject to restrictions on incoming and outgoing SWIFT transfers involving their own overseas bank accounts.

For trade-registered legal entities, identity, authority of representation and company information must be checked against MERSİS, the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette and other relevant official data. Unresolved beneficial ownership or inconsistent information can require termination of the process. Institutions must report information on relevant passport-onboarded customers to MASAK quarterly.

TTL INSIGHT — International brokers, crypto service providers and foreign corporate clients should review onboarding journeys, beneficial-owner evidence, registry data, powers of representation, overseas account arrangements and transaction restrictions. Digital customer acceptance should be designed around regulatory controls rather than convenience alone.
Financial Services, Sanctions & Digital Compliance

Payment and Electronic-Money Institutions Receive Updated Remote KYC Standards

Two instruments published on 4 September 2026 amended the regulatory framework governing payment and electronic-money institutions. The changes clarify the use of biometric verification and electronic identity documents in remote customer identification.

The accompanying systems and data-sharing communiqué establishes more detailed verification procedures, with NFC verification as the primary method for checking an identity document and its embedded data. Where NFC cannot be used, other permitted technologies and security checks apply. The process must include document-integrity and authenticity controls and continuous records. Specified low-risk or non-continuing relationships and certain payment products remain outside the particular remote-identification rules.

Non-Turkish individuals may be identified remotely through an NFC-enabled passport compliant with ICAO 9303, subject to applicable MASAK rules. Both instruments entered into force on publication.

TTL INSIGHT — Payment institutions, fintech groups and embedded-finance providers should update KYC technical specifications, biometric-data governance, retention arrangements, customer consent/privacy documentation and vendor contracts. Whether a particular onboarding flow is permitted depends on the customer's status and the service provided.
Financial Services, Sanctions & Digital Compliance

MASAK Requires Immediate Action on UN Sanctions Listings

MASAK General Communiqué No. 34, published on 23 September 2026, introduces urgent measures relating to persons and entities listed under UN Security Council sanctions regimes covered by Türkiye's terrorism-financing and proliferation-financing legislation.

Listing information is to be announced on MASAK's website following the designated official notification process. Obliged entities covered by the Turkish anti-money-laundering framework must promptly report attempted transactions involving listed persons or organisations to MASAK and take the measures required to facilitate postponement of those transactions. The communiqué entered into force on publication.

The same Official Gazette also published MASAK Communiqué No. 33, increasing specified monetary thresholds in an earlier general communiqué. Those threshold changes concern particular provisions and should not be treated as a blanket increase in every AML reporting limit.

TTL INSIGHT — Banks, payment institutions, crypto businesses and other obliged persons should review sanctions-screening alerts, customer and beneficial-owner matching, escalation paths, recordkeeping and transaction-hold instructions. Screening must address updates to relevant listings, not merely initial customer onboarding.
Financial Services, Sanctions & Digital Compliance

Tax Reporting Rules Extend to Digital Listings and Platform Activity

On 5 September 2026, Tax Procedure Law General Communiqué No. 595 amended Communiqué No. 538, extending tax-information reporting beyond conventional e-commerce to a wider range of online sales, rentals, listings and advertising activity.

The updated framework addresses e-commerce providers and intermediaries as well as relevant access, content, hosting and social-network service providers. In particular, providers publishing advertisements for the sale or rental of movable or immovable property or for goods or services must report specified website, customer-identification or tax, and listing information electronically to the Revenue Administration, on a monthly basis where required.

The amendment entered into force on publication. Reporting scope, format and implementation must be assessed by reference to the Revenue Administration's applicable technical rules and the actual function of each service provider.

TTL INSIGHT — International marketplaces, classified-advertising platforms, property portals and social or hosting platforms operating in Türkiye should map which Turkish reporting rules apply to their business model and verify the data they collect from advertisers. Tax reporting and KVKK transparency, retention and transfer requirements need to be coordinated.
International Trade & Customs

Export-Related Tax, Duty and Fee Exemptions Expanded to Further Rediscount Credits

On 5 September 2026, Presidential Decision No. 11723 and Export Communiqué No. 2026/3 broadened wording in the regime for exemptions from specified taxes, duties and fees in export, transit-trade and foreign-currency-earning transactions.

Previously, the relevant reference covered short-term bill-rediscount credits made available by the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye to Türk Eximbank. The revised wording covers bill-rediscount credits made available to Türk Eximbank and other banks, without the earlier narrow short-term formulation. The changes entered into force on publication.

TTL INSIGHT — Turkish exporters and international groups funding Turkish export operations should identify whether their specific lending arrangement and transaction fall within the exemption regime. Financing documentation, the qualifying activity and the particular taxes or charges must be reviewed; the amendment is not a general exemption for all export-related borrowing.
International Trade & Customs

Definitive Anti-Dumping Duties Imposed on Chinese Solar-Panel Junction Boxes

On 23 September 2026, Communiqué No. 2026/29 concluded a Turkish anti-dumping investigation concerning certain Chinese-origin junction boxes used for solar panels. Definitive duties were introduced on specified products under tariff headings including 8544.42.90.00.11, 8544.60.10.00.11 and, for relevant junction boxes, 8544.60.90.00.00.

The measure is 38.23% of CIF value for the named manufacturers identified in the communiqué and 57.11% for other relevant producers/exporters, according to the applicable product and exporter categorisation. The duties are collected at importation into free circulation. The measure took effect on publication and is generally subject to a five-year term, unless extended through an applicable review process.

TTL INSIGHT — Solar-component importers, EPC contractors, distributors and overseas manufacturers should check tariff classification, origin, producer identity and the precise measure before quoting delivered prices or agreeing supply contracts. A duty applying to a junction box should not be assumed to apply to every solar-panel product.
Food, Life Sciences & Product Compliance

New Food-Flavourings Regulation Introduces Product and Ingredient Restrictions

The Turkish Food Codex Regulation on Flavourings and Food Ingredients with Flavouring Properties, published on 14 September 2026, replaces the former regulatory framework for flavouring substances, source materials, manufacturing, use and labelling.

The Regulation prohibits flavourings and relevant source materials originating from pigs and insects, introduces rules for use of the word “natural”, and restricts flavourings that can be placed on the market. A named source may be used in a natural-flavour claim only when the relevant composition requirements are met, including the 95% source rule in the specified circumstances. Detailed rules also govern business-to-business labelling and safety information.

Existing operators generally have until 31 December 2026 to comply. From that date, ten specifically identified flavouring substances may no longer be newly placed on the market, used in food manufacturing or imported in the cases described by the Regulation. Further restrictions apply to two other substances. Stock and shelf-life exceptions apply in defined circumstances.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign food producers, flavouring manufacturers, brand owners and Turkish importers should conduct an ingredient-level portfolio review before the deadline. Supplier declarations, formulation records, labels, customs documentation and the annexed substance lists should be reconciled. The legal effect on a finished product cannot be determined from its marketing name alone.
Food, Life Sciences & Product Compliance

New Rules for Meat Products and Flavoured Drinking Milk

Two Turkish Food Codex instruments published on 16 September 2026 affect food manufacturers, importers, retailers and restaurant supply chains.

Meat and prepared meat products: Communiqué No. 2026/15 revises composition, production, retail and labelling rules for raw meat, minced meat, prepared meat mixtures and processed meat products. It restricts mixing between meat categories, the use of mechanically separated meat and specified flavourings, and certain product claims, including “100%” claims of the kinds identified by the communiqué. Product-specific protein, fat, salt and other technical limits apply.

Drinking milk: Communiqué No. 2026/19 changes the rules for flavoured drinking milk. It establishes a minimum flavouring-ingredient proportion of 1%, limits added sugar to 5% and total sugar to 9.5%, permits only natural flavourings in the relevant products and introduces front-of-pack labelling obligations. Use of the description “natural” is more narrowly defined.

Existing businesses must generally comply by 31 March 2027, with specified shelf-life rules for products placed on the market before the deadline.

TTL INSIGHT — International food businesses should check recipes, nutrition specifications, manufacturing arrangements, label artwork, translated product claims and existing inventory. The underlying annexes matter: broad descriptions such as “meat product” or “flavoured milk” do not establish compliance without product-category analysis.
Food, Life Sciences & Product Compliance

Medical-Device Notified Bodies Face New Quality, Cost and Timeline Standards

On 16 September 2026, Türkiye published a communiqué governing certain common quality-management and procedural requirements for notified bodies undertaking conformity assessments under its Medical Device Regulation and In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Device Regulation.

The communiqué requires more transparent quotations for conformity assessments, including anticipated costs, potential additions and approximate completion times. Among its procedural benchmarks are 30 days for initial application assessment and contracting, 120 days for quality-management-system audits, 90 days for specified product-verification work and 20 days for certification decisions. Additional requirements address planned device changes, certificate renewal, post-market data and publication of annual average time and cost information.

Most provisions apply from 25 February 2027, with exceptions for existing contracts and certain certificates. The later annual reporting provisions begin on 1 January 2028.

TTL INSIGHT — Overseas medical-device and IVD manufacturers, authorised representatives and Turkish distributors should check certification project timetables, notified-body contracts, cost-change mechanisms and renewal deadlines. The new rules concern notified-body procedures; they do not replace the manufacturer's product compliance duties.
Food, Life Sciences & Product Compliance

Biotechnology Decisions Illustrate the Need for Product-Specific Biosafety Authorisation

On 24 September 2026, the Biosafety Board published two notable decisions. Decision No. 41 approved the extension of validity periods for earlier decisions concerning specified maize events (MIR604, MON863 and T25) and a soybean event (MON87701). The precise new effective period should be checked against the underlying authorisations.

Decision No. 42 authorised Livzym Biyoteknoloji to produce beta-lactoglobulin using a modified Aspergillus oryzae strain identified as LIVZ-114, subject to the express condition that all output be exported. The authorisation runs for ten years and carries biosafety planning, recordkeeping, packaging, transport, waste-management and inspection obligations. Relevant safety and risk-management plans must be submitted within 30 days of publication.

TTL INSIGHT — These decisions demonstrate that biotechnology approvals are strain-, product-, applicant- and use-specific. Foreign biotechnology and food-tech groups should not treat an authorisation granted to another operator as a general permission to manufacture or market comparable materials in Türkiye. Market-entry planning should distinguish domestic use, export-only production and applicable food-law rules.
Employment, Aviation & Digital Infrastructure

Hybrid Working Is Expressly Recognised in the Remote Working Regulation

A regulation published on 25 September 2026 inserted a new paragraph into Article 9 of the Remote Working Regulation.

The new provision expressly permits parties to agree that an employee will perform part of their work at the workplace and part remotely, provided that the days and working hours for each arrangement are specified in the employment contract. The amendment entered into force on publication.

TTL INSIGHT — Foreign-owned Turkish subsidiaries, technology businesses and employers with hybrid teams should review contract templates and existing remote-work addenda. The new express recognition of hybrid arrangements is not a waiver of working-time, occupational safety, information-security, data-protection or other employment obligations.
Employment, Aviation & Digital Infrastructure

New Aviation Safety Occurrence Reporting Framework Published

The Civil Aviation Safety Occurrences Regulation (SHY-OLAY), published on 18 September 2026, establishes new arrangements for reporting, preserving, analysing and following up safety occurrences across civil aviation operations.

Relevant personnel, including flight, air-traffic, ground-handling, maintenance, design and production personnel, must generally submit reports within 72 hours after becoming aware of a reportable occurrence. Service providers must carry out risk assessments and submit preliminary and final reports within the applicable stages, generally 14, 30 and 90 days. The framework requires protected reporting, appropriate confidentiality and defined retention of relevant records.

The regulation enters into force six months after publication, on 18 March 2027. Relevant service providers then have 18 months to establish compliant reporting systems and storage arrangements under the transitional provision.

TTL INSIGHT — Airlines, maintenance organisations, airports, ground-handling companies and aviation manufacturers should map reportable occurrences, responsible personnel, internal escalation, investigation records and the safeguards for reporters. Aviation supply and service contracts should support timely access to necessary safety data.
Employment, Aviation & Digital Infrastructure

Türkiye Sets Out Its 2030 National Smart Cities Strategy

Presidential Circular No. 2026/11, published on 19 September 2026, announced the 2030 National Smart Cities Strategy and Action Plan following the earlier 2020–2023 programme.

The strategy emphasises interoperable municipal services, data-driven and AI-supported decision-making, digital security, resource-efficient infrastructure, environmental sustainability and urban resilience. The Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change coordinates the programme and public authorities and local governments are instructed to implement their relevant responsibilities.

The circular is principally a public-sector policy and implementation framework, rather than a new general permit or licensing rule for every private technology supplier.

TTL INSIGHT — International providers of smart-building systems, transport technology, urban data platforms, cybersecurity, energy management and digital public infrastructure should monitor procurement programmes arising from the strategy. Public-sector contracting, data governance, interoperability, cybersecurity and intellectual-property allocation should be considered together.
Practical next steps

What International Businesses Should Do Next

A September regulatory review should be carried out by legal entity, product and activity, rather than at group level alone. Foreign investors should screen investment and financing eligibility; importers should map tariff and product-classification exposure; manufacturers should examine technical annexes and transition periods; and regulated service providers should document customer identification, screening and reporting processes. Where Turkish operations depend on distributors, contract manufacturers, laboratories or specialist contractors, the contracts should identify who is responsible for each compliance step.
