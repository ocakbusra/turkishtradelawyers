Who Regulates the Animation Industry in Türkiye?

Türkiye does not have one authority for every animation business. The applicable institution depends on the production, distribution and commercial model.

Ministry of Culture and Tourism

The General Directorate of Copyright administers the FSEK framework, including economic rights, registration and producer-related procedures. The Directorate General of Cinema is relevant to support, classification, co-production and film-production matters.

RTÜK

RTÜK regulates television, radio and qualifying on-demand audiovisual media services. A studio making content for a platform is not automatically the operator of a regulated media service; the distinction matters for internet broadcasting licences.

Ministry of Trade

The Ministry and Advertising Board may become relevant to branded animation, advertising films, children’s advertising, influencer campaigns, subscriptions, merchandise and consumer-facing digital sales.

Other authorities

Depending on the project, analysis may also involve tax, customs, competition, personal-data, employment, consumer-protection, public-support and foreign-production rules.

FSEK and Animator Authorship in Türkiye

The principal copyright statute is Law No. 5846 on Intellectual and Artistic Works (Fikir ve Sanat Eserleri Kanunu, or FSEK). An animation may contain separate protected works and rights, including the script, dialogue, character artwork, concept art, storyboard, music, sound recording, illustration, software, animation sequence and final cinematographic work.

FSEK Article 8 is particularly important for animation: for cinematographic works created through animation techniques, the animator is included among the joint authors together with the other contributors identified by the law. A producer should not assume that payment of an invoice or employment status alone answers every copyright question.

Each production should identify the contributors, the rights they create, the rights the producer needs, the rights already owned by the studio and the rights obtained through agreement. The legal analysis should also distinguish authors’ rights, performer rights, producer-related rights and contractual exploitation rights.

Chain of Title and Copyright Transfers

A chain of title shows how the producer obtained the rights required to develop, reproduce, adapt, distribute, perform, communicate, broadcast, stream, localize, merchandise and otherwise exploit the animation.

It may need to cover:

  • animators, directors, screenwriters and dialogue writers;
  • storyboard artists, concept artists, illustrators, character designers and 3D artists;
  • composers, musicians, sound designers and voice actors;
  • employees, freelancers, subcontractors and external animation studios;
  • software, stock assets, fonts, music, recordings, trademarks and underlying properties; and
  • characters, sequels, spin-offs, trailers, games, publishing and promotional materials.

Under FSEK Article 52, transactions concerning economic rights must be in writing and the rights forming the subject of the transaction must be identified separately. A sentence such as “all intellectual property belongs to the producer” may not provide the clarity needed for a financed, distributed or internationally licensed production.

Rights schedules should match the business plan and identify media, territory, duration, languages, exclusivity, sublicensing, platforms, derivative works, approvals, credits, royalties and termination consequences.

Employees, Freelancers, Voice Actors and Music Rights

Animation studios commonly use a mixed workforce. Employee agreements and freelancer agreements should distinguish the creative contribution from the commercial rights needed by the studio. The file should address copyright, economic rights, confidentiality, source and project files, unused material, software, third-party material, credit, portfolio use, warranties and post-termination obligations.

Voice actors may hold performer rights in addition to contractual rights. Voice-over agreements should cover recording, dubbing, editing, localization, streaming, trailers, advertising, games, synthetic or AI-assisted uses and digital replicas where relevant. Consent for one recording or one platform should not be assumed to cover every later exploitation.

Music clearance should identify whether the score is original, commissioned, licensed or adapted. The agreement should cover composition, recording, performers, collecting-society issues, synchronization, public performance, communication to the public, territories, languages and future formats.

Producer Certification and Registration

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism operates a producer certification system for film and phonogram producers. An animation company acting as a film producer should check whether a Yapımcı Sertifikası is required for its model and should plan the application documents early.

Türkiye also has a mandatory registration framework for cinematographic works, including relevant domestic and imported productions. Registration does not create copyright; copyright protection and registration are separate questions. Registration can nevertheless improve evidentiary clarity, identify rights holders and support commercialization, distribution and enforcement.

Before submitting an application, the producer should confirm that the rights documentation, contributor agreements, company information, production details and intended exploitation model are consistent. A gap in the chain of title can affect both a regulatory application and the commercial value of the production.

Animation Film Support and AI Disclosure

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism maintains a dedicated Animation Film Production Support programme for qualifying animation film projects. The published guidance states that applications may be submitted by the director or producer, subject to the relevant conditions, and that projects under the applicable duration threshold should follow the programme-specific rules.

The Ministry’s current application guidance also requires applicants to disclose when AI tools were used to prepare application documents such as a script, synopsis or treatment. That is an application-transparency requirement; it is not a blanket rule deciding who owns every AI-assisted output.

Studios using generative AI should separately review:

  • the tool’s terms, licence and commercial-use restrictions;
  • training-data and third-party material risk;
  • ownership, output and indemnity provisions;
  • confidentiality and client restrictions;
  • voice, likeness and digital-replica permissions; and
  • warranties given to investors, broadcasters, distributors and platforms.

Co-Productions, Distribution and Streaming

International animation projects may be structured as domestic or international co-productions. A co-production agreement should address the underlying property, characters, scripts, assets, financing, budget, recoupment, territories, distribution, streaming, merchandising, approvals, credits, delivery materials, audit rights and ownership after termination.

Where official co-production status or public support is sought, the application may require a synopsis, treatment, screenplay, co-production agreement, revenue and market allocation, artistic and technical contributions, budget, financing plan, schedule and proof of the rights required for commercial exploitation.

Distribution and streaming agreements should identify the rights granted, territory, term, platforms, exclusivity, languages, localization, censorship or editing, sublicensing, minimum guarantees, royalties, reporting, audits, marketing, approvals, delivery standards and dispute resolution. Where the animation company operates its own qualifying on-demand service, the RTÜK internet broadcasting framework should be assessed separately.

Children’s Content, Advertising and Merchandising

Animated content aimed at children requires careful review of content, broadcasting, advertising, consumer and privacy rules. The format may increase appeal to children; it does not reduce the obligations that apply to a product or campaign.

For branded animation, the production agreement should distinguish the studio’s production responsibility from the client’s responsibility for the advertising claim. It should specify who supplies and substantiates claims, clears trademarks and music, obtains product approvals, approves talent, responds to an Advertising Board decision and bears the cost of withdrawal or revision.

Character value often continues after release through toys, books, games, apps, apparel, live events and licensing. Copyright and trademark strategy should therefore be planned together, including ownership, filing, classes, territories, quality control, approvals, royalties and enforcement.

Frequently Asked Questions About Animation Law in Türkiye

Who owns the copyright in an animated film in Türkiye?

Ownership depends on the protected contributions, the authors recognized by Law No. 5846 and the rights obtained through valid agreements. FSEK Article 8 expressly includes the animator among the joint authors of cinematographic works created through animation techniques.

The producer should build a written chain of title covering animators, directors, writers, composers, voice performers and other contributors.

Are animators authors under Turkish copyright law?

Yes. For cinematographic works created through animation techniques, FSEK Article 8 includes the animator among the joint authors.

A general statement that all intellectual property belongs to the producer may not resolve all ownership issues; the relevant economic rights and contributor arrangements should be documented with appropriate formalities.

What is a chain of title for an animation production?

A chain of title is the documentary path showing how a producer obtained the rights required to develop, reproduce, adapt, distribute, communicate, broadcast, stream, merchandise and otherwise exploit an animation.

It may include animator, director, writer, composer, voice actor, freelancer, stock-content, software, character and underlying-property agreements.

Must animation copyright transfers be in writing in Türkiye?

Transactions concerning economic rights under FSEK are subject to specific formal requirements. Article 52 requires written form and separate identification of the rights that are the subject of the transaction.

A detailed agreement should address intended media, territories, languages, duration, exclusivity, sublicensing and derivative uses.

Does an animation producer need a producer certificate in Türkiye?

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism operates a producer certification system for film and phonogram producers. An animation company acting as a film producer should check whether a Yapımcı Sertifikası and related procedures apply to its production and commercialization model.

Mandatory registration requirements for the cinematographic work should also be assessed separately.

Can an animation project receive government support in Türkiye?

Türkiye has a dedicated Animation Film Production Support programme for qualifying animation film projects. The Ministry states that applications may be made by the director or producer, subject to the programme conditions.

Application periods, eligibility and documents should be checked for the relevant year.

Must AI use be disclosed in animation support applications?

The Ministry’s animation support guidance states that AI use in preparing application documents, such as a script, synopsis or treatment, must be expressly disclosed in the relevant application documentation.

This disclosure requirement is separate from the copyright analysis of AI-assisted or AI-generated material, which should be reviewed through the tool terms, inputs, outputs and third-party rights.

Does RTÜK regulate every animation studio in Türkiye?

No. Creating animation for a broadcaster or platform does not automatically make a studio a regulated media service provider.

RTÜK analysis becomes particularly important where the business itself operates a television, radio or qualifying on-demand internet broadcasting service under Law No. 6112 and the related internet broadcasting framework.

What should an animation production agreement cover?

It should match the workflow and address the brief, script, storyboard, milestones, approvals, revisions, delivery, payment, credits, confidentiality, source files, pre-existing assets, third-party materials, economic rights, warranties, liability, cancellation and dispute resolution.

Final-deliverable ownership should be separated from the studio’s underlying tools, libraries and reusable assets.
